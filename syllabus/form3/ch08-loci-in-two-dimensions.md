# Form 3, Chapter 8: Loci in Two Dimensions

## Overview
Locus as a set of points satisfying a condition, first explored through real-life situations and loci in two and three dimensions, then restricted to accurate two-dimensional constructions. Core 2D work covers constant distance from a point (circle), from a straight line (parallel lines), from two points (perpendicular bisector), from two parallel lines (parallel line midway), and from two intersecting lines (angle bisectors), together with intersections of loci and regions satisfying stated conditions.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch8 constructions (perpendicular bisector, angle bisector); Form 2 Ch5 circles; coordinates helpful.

## Topic & subtopic breakdown

| # | Subtopic | Content coverage | Key skills / formulae | Typical question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | Exploration and construction of loci | Explore loci in real-life situations and compare examples in two and three dimensions before formal 2D construction: for example, points at a fixed distance from a point form a circle in a plane and a sphere in space; points at a fixed perpendicular distance from a straight line in a plane form two parallel lines, while the analogous locus around a line in space forms a cylindrical surface. Then describe, sketch, and accurately construct the five standard 2D loci: points at a fixed distance from a point; at a fixed distance from a straight line; equidistant from two fixed points; equidistant from two parallel lines; and equidistant from two intersecting lines. Describe a locus in words from its diagram. | Recognise locus as a set of points satisfying a condition; distinguish a 2D locus from its 3D analogue; use ruler-and-compass constructions for the five standard 2D loci. | Identify a locus from a real context (sprinkler coverage, signal range, safety distance); match 2D and 3D examples; identify/construct/describe a single 2D locus; sketch a locus on a grid. |
| 8.2 | Intersection of loci and regions | Find point(s) satisfying two conditions simultaneously (intersection of two loci); shade a region satisfying conditions such as "closer to A than B", "within 3 cm of O", or "nearer to line PQ than PR"; count or describe points on a grid satisfying combined conditions. State explicitly whether boundary points satisfy each condition. | Intersect loci; translate verbal conditions into the required side or region; determine boundary inclusion from the wording or symbols rather than from line style alone. | Mark intersection points; shade a region defined by 2–3 conditions; find the number of lattice points satisfying conditions. |

## Difficulty strategies

### 8.1
- **Easy**: identify the path traced by a familiar moving object; describe the locus of points 4 cm from O (circle); describe the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points.
- **Medium**: compare the circle formed in a plane with the sphere formed in space; select the appropriate locus for a real-life condition; construct a standard 2D locus accurately (for example, a perpendicular bisector using arcs).
- **Hard**: interpret a less-direct real context and construct two different standard 2D loci in one diagram; describe a compound locus formed from standard loci.
- **Enrichment (not core Form 3 generation)**: constant-distance loci around a line segment, rectangle, or polygonal boundary (offset straight portions joined by arcs), including obstacle-offset paths.

### 8.2
- **Easy**: intersection of a circle and a line locus (mark points).
- **Medium**: equidistant-from-two-points ∩ distance-from-line (bisector meets circle — two intersection points); shade single-condition region.
- **Hard**: region satisfying three conditions (e.g. at least 3 cm from O, closer to AB than AD, and at most 5 cm from O); count grid points in the region; interpret which boundaries are included from the stated conditions.
- **Enrichment/cross-topic**: calculate the area of a locus region such as an annulus or sector-based region (links Form 2 Ch5). Do not use area calculation as a default core Chapter 8 question.
- Distractors: wrong bisector type (angle vs perpendicular); including or excluding a boundary contrary to the stated condition; shading the complementary region; treating a fixed distance from a line segment as though the segment were an infinite straight line.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED for construction and region items** — precise SVG diagrams on grids: fixed points/lines labelled, loci drawn using the convention of the target textbook or paper, construction arcs included when the construction method is assessed, and shaded regions supplied with a key where needed.
- Do not assume that solid means inclusive and dashed means exclusive. Locus construction questions may use broken lines for constructed loci regardless of boundary inclusion. Communicate inclusion or exclusion in the question wording (and symbols where appropriate), and follow the drawing convention of the target paper.
- Grid background strongly recommended (SPM uses square grids).

## Generator notes
- Coordinates make generation robust: place fixed points/lines at lattice points; verify intersection points lie on-grid when the question asks to mark them.
- For region questions choose conditions whose intersection region is non-empty and visually unambiguous on a 10×10 grid.
- Boundary inclusion must be stated unambiguously in the question. Define potentially ambiguous everyday terms rather than relying on them: for example, use "distance at most 3 cm (including points exactly 3 cm away)" or "distance less than 3 cm (excluding points exactly 3 cm away)".
- Generate the real-life and 2D/3D recognition exploration as conceptual work; require accurate ruler-and-compass construction only for the subsequent 2D loci.
- Keep polygon/obstacle offset paths and locus-region area calculations tagged `scope: enrichment`; they must not displace construction, intersection, and region-description items tagged `scope: core`.
- Cross-link: Form 1 Ch8 constructions; Form 2 Ch5 circle area only for enrichment region-area questions; Form 4 Ch6 regions defined by linear inequalities as a later algebraic analogue.
