# Form 3, Chapter 9: Straight Lines

## Overview
Core treatment of straight lines: equation y = mx + c, x- and y-intercepts, gradient from equation/points/intercepts, equation from various givens, points on a line, parallel lines, and the intersection point of two lines. Intersections apply simultaneous linear equations already introduced in Form 1; they are not a new simultaneous-equations standard in this chapter. Perpendicular gradients, perpendicular-line equations, and perpendicular-bisector chains are enrichment rather than Form 3 core. This chapter culminates the coordinate-line work from Form 2 Ch7/8/10 and feeds Form 4 Ch6/7.

## Prerequisites
Form 2 Ch7 (coordinates), Ch10 (gradient); Form 1 Ch6 (linear equations in one and two variables, including simultaneous linear equations by graphing, substitution, and elimination).

## Difficulty strategies

### 9.1
- **Easy**: state m and c from y = 3x − 2; equation from m and c directly; y-intercept reading.
- **Medium**: rearrange 2x + 3y = 12 to find gradient (−2/3) and intercepts; equation from gradient + point; from two points with integer m.
- **Hard**: two points giving fractional gradient; equation from x- and y-intercepts (e.g. through (4, 0) and (0, −6)); find unknowns in y = mx + c given two conditions; parallel-line-through-point before Ch9.2 formally (combined); verify three points collinear via equal gradients.
- Distractors: using x-intercept as c; sign of gradient after rearrangement (divide by coefficient of y with sign); swapping Δx/Δy.

### 9.2
- **Easy (core)**: identify parallel lines (same m).
- **Medium (core)**: find a line through a point parallel to y = 2x + 1; rearrange a given equation before comparing gradients.
- **Hard (core)**: find k such that two lines are parallel; combine a parallel-line equation with an intersection.
- **Enrichment only**: negative reciprocal computation (m = 2 ⇒ m⊥ = −½); a perpendicular line through a point; find k such that two lines are perpendicular. A perpendicular-bisector chain combining midpoint, perpendicular gradient, and a line equation must be explicitly tagged `scope: enrichment` and must not appear in core Form 3 generation.
- Distractors: using an intercept instead of gradient when testing parallelism; rearrangement sign errors. For enrichment perpendicular items, using the same reciprocal without negation.

### 9.3
- **Easy**: read or confirm a graphical intersection; substitution where one equation is y = … and values are small positive integers.
- **Medium**: elimination requiring scaling one equation; negative/fraction solutions.
- **Hard**: both equations need scaling (2x + 3y = 12, 3x − 2y = 5); word problems (two items' prices from two shopping combinations; phone plans break-even — find usage where costs equal). An intersection-of-perpendicular-lines chain is enrichment, and adding distance from the origin makes it a cumulative multi-chapter enrichment task.
- Distractors: elimination sign errors (subtracting negative); substituting into a rearranged wrong equation; fractional answer rounding.

## Generator notes
- Generate lines from chosen m (integer or simple fraction ±1/2, ±2/3, …) and integer intercepts so all key features are lattice points.
- For enrichment perpendicular pairs, pick m from {±1, ±2, ±½, ±3, ±⅓, ±2/3, ±3/2, ±3/4, ±4/3} so the negative reciprocal stays clean; never select this generator for core Form 3 items.
- Intersection questions: choose intersection point first, then build two lines through it.
- Treat substitution and elimination here as prerequisite methods from Form 1 Ch6. The Form 3 learning target is relating two line equations to their common intersection and applying that relationship, not introducing simultaneous equations for the first time.
- Word problems must yield positive sensible answers (prices > 0).
- Cross-link: Form 1 Ch6 (simultaneous linear equations); Form 2 Ch7/10; Form 4 Ch6 (linear inequalities in two variables — region relative to these lines); Form 4 Ch7 (motion graphs use gradient/area); Form 4 Ch1 (intersection with quadratics conceptually).
