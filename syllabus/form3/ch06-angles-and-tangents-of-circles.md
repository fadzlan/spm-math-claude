# Form 3, Chapter 6: Angles and Tangents of Circles

## Overview
Circle geometry theorems: angles at centre vs circumference, angles in the same segment, angle in a semicircle, cyclic quadrilaterals; tangent properties (perpendicular to radius, equal tangents from an external point, angle between tangent and chord), common tangents, and combined reasoning problems. Application items are often diagram-based, while theorem identification, reason statements, and descriptions of constructions can also be assessed in text or tables.

## Prerequisites
Form 2 Ch5 (circle parts); Form 1 Ch8–9 (angles, triangles, quadrilaterals).

## Difficulty strategies

### 6.1
- **Easy**: central 80° ⇒ inscribed 40° (single theorem).
- **Medium**: inscribed given ⇒ the corresponding minor or reflex central angle; same-segment equal angles chain; semicircle right angle used with triangle sum.
- **Hard**: 3+ step deductions combining central angle, isosceles radius triangles, and angle sums; find multiple unknowns x, y; angles given algebraically ((2x)°, solve); semicircle + tangent mixed diagrams.
- Distractors: halving instead of doubling (direction errors); selecting the minor central angle when the intercepted arc is major; assuming equal angles merely because they share chord endpoints even though their vertices lie in opposite segments.

### 6.2
- **Easy**: opposite angles 180° (one given).
- **Medium**: exterior = interior opposite; combined with central theorem (find centre angle then quadrilateral angle).
- **Hard**: algebraic opposite angles ((3x + 10)° + (x − 20)° = 180°); cyclic quadrilateral with parallel chords/tangent mixed; multi-unknown full-solution items.
- Distractors: using 360° pairing wrongly; assuming any quadrilateral in/near a circle is cyclic.

### 6.3
- **Easy**: tangent-radius right angle used once (find angle in triangle).
- **Medium**: equal tangents from external point (find perimeter/length); Pythagoras with OT² = r² + tangent².
- **Hard**: angle between tangent and chord chained with cyclic quadrilateral or central angle; kite formed by two tangents + two radii (angle sums); algebraic angles; length and angle in one problem.
- Distractors: alternate segment angle placed in the wrong segment; assuming any line touching the circle is a tangent without perpendicularity; forgetting the 90° at contact.

### 6.4
- **Easy**: identify whether a shown line is a common external tangent, a common internal tangent, or not a common tangent.
- **Medium**: use the two perpendicular radii at the contact points with quadrilateral or straight-line angle facts; construct a permitted common tangent and mark both contact points.
- **Hard**: combine a common tangent with radii, centre-to-centre lines, Pythagoras, or tangent lengths in a multi-step problem. Supply all information needed; do not infer tangency from appearance alone.
- Distractors: treating a secant as a tangent; marking only one contact point; assuming every pair of circles has the same number or type of common tangents.

## Visual aids
- **Usually required for application and construction items** — precise circle SVGs: centre marked (O), points on circumference labelled, angle arcs, radii drawn where needed, tangent lines touching at exactly one point with each contact point labelled, and auxiliary radii visually distinguished. Diagrams must be constraint-consistent with the answer key. State “not drawn to scale” when students should reason rather than measure.
- **Text/table variants are valid** for naming a theorem, choosing the correct reason for a stated deduction, completing a reason column, detecting an invalid inference from a verbal configuration, or ordering/explaining construction steps. Wording must specify the centre, cyclic order or segment, chord endpoints, contact points and tangency facts sufficiently to make the answer unique.
- **Construction/verification items** may use ruler-and-compass steps, a supplied construction sequence, or dynamic-geometry measurements. Require students to construct the relevant radii/chords/tangents, record the right angles, equal lengths or equal angles being tested, and state the conjecture supported. Repeating the construction with more than one configuration strengthens empirical verification; do not present measurement alone as proof.

## Generator notes
- Build diagrams backwards from chosen answer angles so all marked values are mutually consistent (write the constraint chain: central–inscribed, opposite-sum-180, tangent-90).
- Represent each circle by an ordered list of points and explicit arcs. For ∠ACB, the intercepted arc AB is the arc not containing C; generate ∠AOB as twice ∠ACB only when ∠AOB represents that same arc. Use an explicit reflex-angle flag and a three-point arc name where needed. Never obtain a reflex case by doubling and then silently replacing the result by the smaller angle.
- For same-segment items, verify that both circumference vertices lie in the same segment determined by the same chord, so that both angles intercept the same opposite arc. If the vertices lie in opposite segments, use the cyclic/supplementary relationship instead of equality.
- Phrase the alternate-segment result as “angle between tangent and chord” where appropriate, and validate which of the two tangent rays and which chord ray define the stated angle.
- For common-tangent items, generate the circle positions first and validate that the requested tangent type exists. Store both contact points and enforce perpendicularity of both contact radii; do not rely on a visually near-tangent line.
- Include reason-giving items: students may state the theorem name or an accepted equivalent mathematical reason. Define accepted variants in the mark scheme rather than requiring one exact phrase.
