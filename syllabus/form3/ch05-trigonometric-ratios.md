# Form 3, Chapter 5: Trigonometric Ratios in Right-Angled Triangles

## Overview
Introduces sine, cosine and tangent of acute angles in right-angled triangles; explores how the ratios change as the acute angle changes, including endpoint values at 0° and 90°; establishes `tan θ = sin θ / cos θ`; uses the ratios to find unknown sides and angles; develops exact 30°/45°/60° values; and applies the ratios to elevation, depression, and two- and three-dimensional geometric objects. A major SPM chapter bridging geometry and real measurement; extended in Form 5 Ch6.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch13 Pythagoras; Form 1 Ch8 angles; calculator use with sin/cos/tan and inverse functions.

## Difficulty strategies

### 5.1
- **Easy**: state the three ratios from a 3-4-5 triangle with angle marked; recall one exact value, such as `sin 30° = 1/2`, `tan 45° = 1`, or `cos 60° = 1/2`.
- **Medium**: ratios from triangles needing Pythagoras first (two sides given); identify which ratio to use for a given side/angle pair; complete or interpret a table for several acute angles and describe that sine and tangent increase while cosine decreases; complete a special-angle table or evaluate a one- or two-term exact expression.
- **Hard**: given `sin θ = 3/5`, find `cos θ` and `tan θ` by constructing a right triangle; compare ratio values across acute angles without a calculator; derive `tan θ = sin θ / cos θ` by dividing the side-ratio definitions; explain why tangent is undefined at 90°; derive exact values from a special right triangle or evaluate multi-term exact expressions and simplify surds, recognising equivalent forms such as `1/√3` and `√3/3`.

### 5.2
- **Easy**: find side where unknown is the numerator (tan 40° = x/10).
- **Medium**: unknown in denominator (cos 35° = 8/x ⇒ x = 8/cos 35°); find angle from two sides with inverse ratio; rounding to 1–2 d.p. or nearest degree as instructed.
- **Hard**: two linked right triangles (flagpole on building; two elevations from different distances — find height via two equations, though at KSSM usually sequential not simultaneous); angle of elevation/depression diagrams with horizontal reference lines; combined Pythagoras + trigonometry; and 3D geometric-object problems in which the solver must first extract a right triangle from a face or cross-section (for example, find a cuboid's base diagonal before using a vertical section to find an angle or space diagonal). Real contexts include ladders, ramps, kites, and a ship viewed from a lighthouse.
- Distractors: wrong ratio selection (sin vs cos when angle's position changes); using the angle at the wrong vertex; elevation/depression confusion (depression measured from horizontal, not vertical); calculator in wrong mode (state degree mode assumption); premature rounding in two-step problems.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** — labelled right-triangle SVGs with right-angle marker, marked angle θ, side labels/unknowns. Elevation/depression questions **REQUIRE** scene diagrams with dashed horizontal reference lines and angle arcs at the correct vertex.
- Ratio-change explorations may use a table or a family of similar-hypotenuse right triangles; do not use a graph extending beyond 0°–90° as if angles outside this chapter's scope were required.
- Two-triangle composite diagrams for Hard. For 3D items, draw the solid with unambiguous vertices and highlight or separately show the right-triangle face/cross-section used in the solution; do not infer right angles from appearance alone.

## Generator notes
- Generate triangles from chosen angle + one side; compute others with trig and round consistently (state rounding in the question, typically 2 d.p. for lengths, nearest degree or 1 d.p. for angles).
- In the right-triangle definition, `θ` is acute. Treat 0° and 90° as endpoint values obtained by extending the observed ratio patterns to limiting degenerate triangles, not as interior angles of a non-degenerate right triangle: `sin 0° = 0`, `cos 0° = 1`, `tan 0° = 0`, `sin 90° = 1`, `cos 90° = 0`, and `tan 90°` is undefined because division by zero is undefined.
- For ratio-change items, sample ordered angles within the same domain and calculate at sufficient internal precision. Never imply that tangent has a finite value at 90°.
- For special-angle questions use only 30°/45°/60° and require exact surd/fraction answers unless a decimal is explicitly requested. Store a canonical simplified, rationalised answer, but validate answers by exact algebraic equivalence rather than string matching: for example, accept both `1/√3` and `√3/3` for `tan 30°`. Also accept equivalent unreduced fractions only when the prompt does not explicitly require simplest form. Do not accept a rounded decimal when an exact value is requested.
- Ensure denominator-unknown questions (x in denominator) appear from Medium — key skill differentiator.
- Elevation = depression alternate-angle fact must be reflected in diagram geometry (alternate angles between parallels).
- Build every 3D instance from a consistent coordinate or solid model. Verify all face diagonals, cross-section lengths, space diagonals, and right angles before rendering, and ensure the labelled angle belongs to the extracted right triangle used by the keyed solution.
