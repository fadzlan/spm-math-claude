# Form 3, Chapter 3: Consumer Mathematics: Savings and Investments, Credit and Debt

## Overview
Personal finance: savings and investment products, risk–return–liquidity comparisons, simple and compound interest, return on investment (ROI), and responsible credit/debt management. Contexts include conventional and Islamic banking, loans, hire purchase, credit cards, currency exchange, and online purchases. Questions may assess concepts and financial decisions as well as monetary computation. Foundation for Form 4 Ch10 and Form 5 Ch3–4.

## Prerequisites
Percentages (primary + Form 1); Form 1 Ch4 rates; Form 2 Ch3 formula substitution.

## Difficulty strategies

### 3.1
- **Easy**: match a saver’s need to a savings account, fixed deposit, or current account using stated features; simple interest for one year; maturity value `P + I` with integers; identify the more liquid or lower-risk option from a supplied comparison.
- **Medium**: multi-year simple interest; compound interest annually over 2–3 years (stepwise or by formula); ROI computation; compare simple and compound returns for the same `P`, `r`, and `t`; compare shares, unit trusts, and real estate from a supplied risk–return–liquidity table; calculate a stated Islamic banking profit/return.
- **Hard**: reverse problems (find `P` given `MV` and `r`; find an integer `t` by listing/trials); semi-annual compounding; dividend and rental-return computations; two-option comparison requiring calculations plus a justified recommendation that weighs risk, return, and liquidity; find a rate by an explicitly permitted trial method.
- Distractors: applying simple interest each year instead of compounding; forgetting to convert a percentage to a decimal; treating months as years; using gross maturity value rather than net gain in ROI; assuming the highest return is automatically the best choice despite the investor’s risk or liquidity needs.

### 3.2
- **Easy**: total repayment = principal + flat interest; monthly instalment division.
- **Medium**: hire purchase (cash price versus deposit plus instalments); loan with a supplied flat rate and tenure; credit-card statement with one clearly stated finance charge or fee; convert currency using a bank’s stated buying/selling convention; determine an online-purchase total including delivery, platform, conversion, or transaction fees.
- **Hard**: credit-card statement with a fully stated minimum-payment rule, payment order, finance-charge basis, and late fee; compare bank loan and hire purchase total cost with different supplied rates/tenures; compare two online-payment or currency-conversion options; combine a numerical decision with an explanation of security, responsible card use, or likely effect on creditworthiness.
- Distractors: computing interest on the original price instead of the financed balance after a deposit; applying a minimum-payment rule incorrectly; omitting a deposit or fee from total cost; using the wrong side of a currency quotation; applying a payment before rather than after interest contrary to the stated order; treating available credit as income.

## Visual aids
- **OPTIONAL** — product-feature tables, risk–return–liquidity matrices, repayment/comparison tables, exchange-rate quotations, online checkout summaries, and simplified credit-card statements. Tables are the recommended representation when several products, fees, or transactions must be compared. Charts are not standard.

## Generator notes
- Each item must be self-contained. State every interest/profit/dividend rate, whether it is annual or monthly, the time period, compounding frequency, fee, tax or service charge, exchange-rate convention, transaction/payment order, and rounding rule that the solution needs. Do not rely on current market rates, unstated bank practices, or a supposedly standard credit-card rule.
- Use `r` as a decimal in `I = Prt` and `MV = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)`, where `n` is the number of compounding periods per year and `t` is in years. For a displayed percentage rate, explicitly require conversion to a decimal or provide an equivalent percentage-form calculation.
- State whether money is rounded only in the final answer or at each transaction/compounding stage. If stage rounding is required, state that the rounded value is carried forward. Normally express final RM amounts to the nearest sen.
- Rates and product/card features should be plausible within the fictional item but must not be presented as timeless real-world facts. Do not name a real institution unless its relevant data are supplied and dated.
- For Islamic banking contexts, use neutral terms such as *profit* or *return* and supply the exact calculation model (for example, a stated profit rate applied as simple return or a supplied maturity-value formula). Do not silently substitute the conventional-interest formula or require knowledge of a particular contract structure.
- Responsible-use and security items may cover paying on time and preferably in full, staying within an affordable limit, checking statements, protecting PIN/OTP/card details, using secure payment channels, recognising suspicious messages, reporting loss or fraud promptly, and understanding stated charges. Never ask learners to reveal actual financial credentials.
- Creditworthiness questions must base conclusions on information given in the item, such as repayment history, outstanding debt, or repeated late payments. Avoid claiming a universal score change or approval outcome.
- In currency exchange, define the quotation direction and whether the customer is buying or selling the foreign currency. For online purchases, state the displayed currency, conversion rate, delivery cost, discounts, duties/taxes (if any), platform/bank charges, and the order in which they apply.
- Comparison questions should have a defensible answer under stated criteria. If a choice depends on personal priorities, require a justified answer and specify accepted reasoning instead of forcing a spurious single “best” product.
- Compound interest is usually annual at this level. If another frequency is used, define `n` and provide either `MV = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)` or an equally clear instruction.
- Validate generated items by independently recomputing all balances and totals, checking that fees and payments occur in the stated order, and confirming that the recommendation follows the stated financial priorities.
