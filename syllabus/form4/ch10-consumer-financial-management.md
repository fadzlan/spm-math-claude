# Form 4, Chapter 10: Consumer Mathematics: Financial Management

## Overview
Personal financial management through the full management cycle: set short- and long-term financial goals, evaluate current financial status, create a financial plan, carry out the plan, and review/revise it. SMART goals, needs and wants, feasibility, and decision-making are core. Income, expenditure, budgets, cash flow, and surplus/deficit calculations support this cycle. This chapter continues Form 3 Ch3 and mostly uses structured word problems with tables.

## Prerequisites
Form 3 Ch3 (interest, credit); percentage and money arithmetic.

## Difficulty strategies

- **Easy**: identify or order stages in the financial-management cycle; distinguish short- and long-term goals; identify whether a goal is SMART; compute total income/expenses and cash flow from a given table; classify fixed vs variable expenses.
- **Medium**: evaluate current financial status from a budget; calculate a monthly savings target for a goal (goal ÷ months, with interest explicitly ignored); identify a deficit and compute the reduction needed; complete a partial budget table; select and justify an action to carry out or revise a plan.
- **Hard (core)**: complete a multi-part cycle: set or interpret a goal, evaluate income/expenses/assets/liabilities supplied, create a feasible plan, explain how it will be carried out, then review and revise it after a stated change. Quantify achievability, margin, or shortfall; adjust specified expenses or contingency allocations and re-evaluate.
- **Hard (cross-topic enrichment only)**: compare salary and commission job offers using fully supplied break-even data, or incorporate compound savings growth for a long-term goal using a supplied formula and all rates/conventions. Do not use these to assess unprovided compound-interest or algebraic break-even knowledge.
- Distractors: treating annual figures as monthly (convert!); excluding loan instalments from expenses; percentage cut applied to the wrong category; ignoring one-off expenses in monthly flow.

## Visual aids

## Generator notes
- Assign item metadata `scope: core|supporting prerequisite|enrichment`; do not make enrichment automatic merely because an item is Hard.
- Keep all amounts in RM and state the required rounding; use 2 d.p. for final currency answers where appropriate. Make monthly vs annual conversion an explicit step at Medium+.
- Ensure plan-evaluation questions have a clear verdict (achievable with margin or shortfall of a computable amount).
- Core plan-evaluation items should assess the whole cycle, not reduce the chapter to cash-flow arithmetic. When asking how a plan is carried out or reviewed, provide an observable action, review date/interval, or changed circumstance from which a definite judgement can be made.
- Cash-flow arithmetic is supporting prerequisite content: connect every substantial calculation to evaluating, creating, carrying out, or revising a financial plan.
- Reuse Form 3 Ch3 loan/savings calculations only in items tagged `enrichment`, unless every required formula and datum is supplied. Likewise, tag job-offer break-even questions `enrichment` and fully specify salary and commission rules.
- Contexts: fresh graduate budget, family plan, saving for car deposit/holiday/studies, side income.
- Cross-link: Form 3 Ch3 (interest/instalments); Form 5 Ch3 insurance and Ch4 taxation add expense/income components to plans.
