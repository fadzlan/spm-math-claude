# Form 4, Chapter 7: Graphs of Motion

## Overview
Interprets and analyses distance-time and speed-time graphs: gradients (speed/acceleration), areas under speed-time graphs (distance), average speed over whole journeys, and describing motion in words from graphs. Applies Form 2 Ch9 (speed/acceleration) and Form 3 Ch9 (gradient) in a graphical setting.

## Prerequisites
Form 2 Ch9 (speed, acceleration); Form 3 Ch9 (gradient); area of trapezium/triangle (Form 1 Ch10).

## Difficulty strategies

### 7.1 Distance-time
- **Easy**: read distance/time; speed of one straight segment.
- **Medium**: multi-stage graph (out, stop, return) — speed each straight stage, stationary duration; average speed for the whole journey (return included — total path length ÷ total elapsed time, with the stop included in elapsed time; traps: use net displacement or omit the rest interval).
- **Hard**: two travellers on one graph (when/where they meet — intersection); find time when distance between them is k; piecewise graph with a curved segment recognised qualitatively as changing speed; construct graph from worded journey then answer questions. Do not request a numerical instantaneous speed from a curved segment unless a tangent, estimation procedure, or other method is supplied.
- Distractors: distance from the reference point vs total distance travelled confusion on return legs; average speed using mean of stage speeds; gradient sign interpreted as negative speed. On a distance-from-reference-point graph, a negative gradient means motion toward the reference point; speed is the magnitude of the gradient and is not negative.

### 7.2 Speed-time
- **Easy**: acceleration from one sloped segment; distance for rectangle portion.
- **Medium**: total distance over a trapezium-shaped graph (0 → accelerate → uniform → decelerate → 0); uniform-speed duration; deceleration magnitude.
- **Hard**: unknown parameter from total distance (graph with speed v unknown, total distance given — solve area equation in v, possibly linear equation); two-part (find v then acceleration); comparing two vehicles' graphs; convert units (m/s graph, distance in km) — unit trap; find speed at a time on a sloped segment (linear interpolation).
- Distractors: area mis-decomposed (trapezium vs rectangle+triangle); using speed × total time instead of area; unit mismatch (h vs s); acceleration = speed/time of wrong segment.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** — piecewise-linear SVG graphs with labelled axes (distance km vs time h; speed m/s vs time s), grid lines, key points at clear lattice coordinates, dashed projection lines to axes optional. Generate the graph from a stage list internally so all derived quantities (gradients, areas) are consistent.
- Two-object questions need both graphs on one axes pair (different styles + legend).

## Generator notes
- Internal model: list of stages (duration, start/end speed or distance) — derive graph points, gradients, areas; choose numbers so key points are integers and areas clean at Easy/Medium; allow one rounding at Hard.
- For a whole-journey average-speed question, sum the distance travelled in every moving stage and divide by the full elapsed time from departure to completion, including any rests. If a different interval is intended, state it explicitly.
- Treat a decreasing straight segment on a distance-from-reference-point graph as travel toward the reference point and report its speed as the magnitude of the gradient. Curved distance-time segments support qualitative conclusions about changing speed only, unless the question supplies an appropriate numerical method.
- State units on axes explicitly; include unit-conversion variants at Hard.
- Example question formats: "Diagram shows a speed-time graph of a car for T seconds… total distance 480 m, find v" and "calculate the rate of change of speed in the last 5 s".
