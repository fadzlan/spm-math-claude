# Form 1, Chapter 1: Rational Numbers

## Overview
The first chapter of secondary-school mathematics formalises number work from primary school in this sequence: integers, arithmetic operations and laws involving integers, positive and negative fractions, positive and negative decimals, and finally rational numbers. It develops number-line representation, comparison, ordering, exact computation and problem solving. These skills form a computational foundation for later chapters.

## Prerequisites
Primary-school whole numbers, positive fractions and decimals, place value, and the four basic operations.

## Difficulty strategies

Difficulty must remain within the stated scope. Increase conceptual demand, representation shifts, number complexity or reasoning steps rather than importing later-form content.

### 1.1 Integers
- **Easy**: identify positive and negative quantities or locate integers on a fully labelled number line.
- **Medium**: compare or order four to five integers, including zero, or infer values from evenly spaced ticks.
- **Hard**: combine contextual interpretation with an incompletely labelled number line or justify an ordering involving close negative values.
- Distractors: confusing magnitude with value, reversing the order of negative integers, or treating zero as positive or negative.

### 1.2 Integer operations and laws
- **Easy**: two small integers and one operation; direct identification of a stated arithmetic law.
- **Medium**: three terms, two operations or brackets; negative results; use a law to simplify a calculation.
- **Hard**: several operations with nested grouping or a missing value; require the student to select and explain an efficient law-based strategy.
- Distractors: subtracting a negative incorrectly; applying sign rules for multiplication to addition; ignoring brackets; incorrectly assuming subtraction or division is commutative or associative.

### 1.3 Fractions
- **Easy**: like denominators, unit fractions, or points on a clearly subdivided number line.
- **Medium**: unlike denominators up to 12, mixed numbers, negatives, two operations, or ordering three to four fractions.
- **Hard**: combined operations with brackets, several signed fractions, or a number-line/comparison task requiring equivalent fractions before ordering.
- Distractors: `1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5`; forgetting to invert the divisor; mishandling the sign; comparing denominators or numerators alone.

### 1.4 Decimals
- **Easy**: tenths or hundredths, one operation, or location on a fully labelled number line.
- **Medium**: different numbers of decimal places, negatives, two operations, or ordering four decimals.
- **Hard**: combined operations with brackets or a contextual problem requiring careful interpretation of a negative result, without unnecessary rounding.
- Distractors: treating `0.13 < 0.128`; misaligning place values; dropping a negative sign; rounding during an intermediate step.

### 1.5 Rational numbers
- **Easy**: recognise integers, simple fractions and terminating decimals as rational; compare three values with familiar equivalents.
- **Medium**: order four to five mixed representations, including negatives, or evaluate a mixed integer/fraction/decimal expression.
- **Hard**: locate a rational number between two given values, reason across several representations, or solve a multi-stage core problem with a unique interpretation.
- Distractors: treating `-2/3` as greater than `-1/2`; comparing surface forms rather than values; using a rounded decimal as though it were exact.

### 1.5E Enrichment/context
- Do not use recurring-decimal conversion to raise the difficulty of a core item. Tag the whole item `scope: enrichment` if that conversion is assessed.
- Savings, budgets, temperature, elevation and transactions may contextualise core arithmetic. A lengthy or separate financial-literacy investigation must be tagged `scope: enrichment` or `scope: context` as appropriate.

## Visual aids
- 1.1: **RECOMMENDED** — a horizontal or vertical integer number line with evenly spaced ticks and unambiguous labels.
- 1.3: **RECOMMENDED** — a number line partitioned using a common denominator; include negative fractions and zero where relevant.
- 1.4: **RECOMMENDED** — a number line whose tick interval and decimal place value are explicit; include negative decimals where relevant.
- 1.5: **RECOMMENDED** for locating or ordering tasks — derive every plotted point from the same exact values used by the solver.
- Operations without a spatial interpretation: **NONE**. Context problems may use a compact table, thermometer or vertical scale only when it supplies necessary information.

For every number line, store exact endpoint, interval, tick and point values as semantic data. Check label collisions, tick consistency and print legibility; do not rely on apparent visual position as the answer key.

## Generator notes
- Attach scope metadata to every item: `core` for rows 1.1-1.5, and `enrichment` or `context` for row 1.5E. A hard core item must remain within core Form 1 content.
- Generate the exact mathematical values and solution first, then render wording, diagram and options from that shared model.
- Keep fraction answers in lowest terms unless another representation is requested. Validate that denominators and divisors are non-zero.
- Distinguish exact conversion from approximation. A terminating conversion may use equality, for example `3/8 = 0.375`. If a value is rounded, use `approximately equal to`/`≈` or wording such as “correct to 2 decimal places”; never write equality after rounding.
- Do not round intermediate values unless the question explicitly requires it. Apply the specified rounding once, to the final answer, and independently verify the rounded result and any accepted tolerance.
- When comparing mixed representations, use exact fractions or exact terminating decimals internally. Do not decide order from display-rounded values. Reject an item if displayed values become equal after required rounding or if the intended order is ambiguous.
- For MCQs, compute each distractor from a documented misconception and reject duplicate options. Useful traps include `(−a) − (−b)` sign errors, failing to invert a fraction divisor, adding fraction numerators and denominators, and place-value comparison errors.
- Use Malaysian contexts naturally (for example RM, temperature and elevation), with realistic units and enough information for one unambiguous numerical model. Treat “saving habit” only as context or enrichment, not as a separate core strand.
- Independently solve each generated item and reject it if the answer is non-unique, a divisor is zero, notation is ambiguous, units are inconsistent, a diagram disagrees with the values, or the requested representation/precision is not honoured.
