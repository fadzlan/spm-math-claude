# Form 1, Chapter 5: Algebraic Expressions

## Overview
First contact with algebra: variables whose values vary or remain fixed in a given context, algebraic terms, expressions, evaluating expressions, like/unlike terms, and simplifying expressions by combining like terms. The gateway chapter for all subsequent algebra (equations, inequalities, expansion/factorisation, formulae).

## Prerequisites
Four operations on integers and fractions (Ch1); squares/cubes notation (Ch3).

## Topic & subtopic breakdown

| # | Subtopic | Content coverage | Key skills / formulae | Typical question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Variables and algebraic expressions | Meaning of variable and constant; distinguishing a variable whose value varies from one whose value is fixed in a given context; algebraic term (coefficient, variable); writing expressions from word descriptions ("3 more than twice x"); generalising repeated multiplication of the same variable as a power. | Translate words ↔ algebra; `a × a = a²`, `a × a × a = a³`, and in general repeated multiplication gives `aⁿ`. | Write an expression for a quantity; identify quantities that vary or are fixed in context; identify coefficient/variable; state number of terms; rewrite repeated multiplication using powers and vice versa. |
| 5.2 | Algebraic expressions involving basic arithmetic operations | Adding/subtracting algebraic terms; multiplying/dividing one algebraic term by one algebraic term; like and unlike terms; simplifying expressions (collecting like terms); evaluating expressions by substitution. | `3x + 2x = 5x`; `2x × 3y = 6xy`; `12a² ÷ 4a = 3a`; substitution. | Simplify `4p − 2p + 5`; simplify `2x × 3y`; evaluate `2a² − b` when `a=3`, `b=−2`; form a perimeter expression from algebraic side lengths. |

## Difficulty strategies

### 5.1 Forming expressions
- **Easy**: one operation ("5 less than m" → m − 5).
- **Medium**: distinguish varying and fixed quantities in a context; use two operations with order ("twice the sum of x and 3" → `2(x + 3)`); use money contexts (cost of `p` pens at RM `q` each).
- **Hard**: expressions from geometric contexts (perimeter of rectangle with sides `(2a+3)` and `(a−1)`); translate two-variable descriptions; generalise repeated multiplication such as `p × p × q × p` to `p³q` and reverse the representation.

### 5.2 Simplifying and evaluating
- **Easy**: two like terms, positive coefficients (5a − 2a).
- **Medium**: 3–4 terms with mixed signs and two variables (`4x − 3y + x + 5y`); multiply one term by one term (`3p × 2q`); divide one monomial by one monomial (`12a² ÷ 4a`).
- **Hard (Form 1 core)**: fractional coefficients (`½x − ⅓x`); substitution with negative numbers and powers (`a = −2` in `a³ − 2a`); multi-step collection of like terms where unlike terms and sign handling are the traps. Multiplication and division must still be one-term-by-one-term.
- **Cumulative enrichment / Form 2 preview**: distribution followed by collection across multiple brackets, for example `3(2a − 1) + 2(a + 4)`. Tag these items `scope: enrichment` and `form: 2`; do not generate them as core Form 1 questions.
- Distractors: combining unlike terms (`2x + 3y = 5xy`); sign errors when collecting signed terms; coefficient×power confusion (`2a²` evaluated as `(2a)²`); `3p × 2q = 5pq`. Use `4x − (x − 3)` only in enrichment items that assess bracket expansion.

## Visual aids
- **NONE** for symbolic manipulation.
- **RECOMMENDED** for geometry-linked forming-expression questions: simple labelled diagram (rectangle with algebraic side lengths).

## Generator notes
- Use single lowercase letters; keep coefficient magnitudes ≤ 12 at Easy/Medium.
- In contextual variable questions, state the situation clearly enough for students to decide which quantities may change and which variable has a fixed value for that situation. A letter is not inherently "varying" or "fixed"; its role depends on the context.
- Include conversions in both directions between repeated multiplication and index notation. Use positive whole-number exponents and repeated variable factors for this Form 1 generalisation.
- For core Form 1 multiplication and division, each operand must be a single algebraic term (monomial). Do not introduce distribution, products of multi-term expressions, or algebraic fractions.
- For substitution questions at Hard, always include at least one negative value — sign handling is the core skill.
- Perimeter contexts can use algebraic side lengths such as `(3x + 1)` cm and `(2x − 4)` cm; generate the expression without requiring multi-bracket expansion for a core Form 1 item.
- Cross-link: feeds Ch6/Ch7 (equations and inequalities), Form 2 Ch2 expansion/factorisation, Form 2 Ch3 formulae.
