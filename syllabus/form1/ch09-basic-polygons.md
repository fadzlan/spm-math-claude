# Form 1, Chapter 9: Basic Polygons

## Overview
Introduces polygons and their properties: identifying, drawing, and naming polygons; relating their sides, vertices, and diagonals; and studying triangles (types and angle sum) and quadrilaterals (types and properties). Bridges angle work (Ch8) to formal polygon theory in Form 2 Ch4.

## Prerequisites
Angles (Ch8).

## Difficulty strategies

### 9.1
- **Easy**: name a polygon with n sides (n ≤ 8); state or match its number of vertices; name a pictured polygon by reading consecutive labelled vertices.
- **Medium**: complete a table of sides, vertices, diagonals from one vertex, and total diagonals; draw and label a polygon satisfying a given side count; identify all valid names of a labelled polygon for a specified starting vertex.
- **Hard**: use the sides–vertices–diagonals relationships in reverse (for example, find the number of sides from the total number of diagonals), restricting generated values to integers corresponding to valid polygons; explain why a proposed vertex sequence is not a valid name.
- **Supporting/enrichment only**: classify convex/concave or regular/irregular polygons, with justification. Do not use these classifications as the main source of core difficulty.

### 9.2 Triangles
- **Easy**: angle sum with two known angles.
- **Medium**: exterior-angle theorem; isosceles triangle with one angle given (decide base vs apex); classify from three angles.
- **Hard**: algebraic angles ((2x)°, (x+10)°, (3x−10)° sum to 180°); exterior angle as algebraic expression equal to sum of two interior opposites; two connected triangles sharing a side; angles combined with parallel lines (Ch8 cross-link).
- Distractors: exterior = adjacent interior; isosceles wrong angle pairing; forgetting 180° total.

### 9.3 Quadrilaterals
- **Easy**: sum to 360° with three known angles; name shape from properties.
- **Medium**: use specific properties (parallelogram opposite angles; kite one pair of opposite angles equal; trapezium co-interior).
- **Hard**: composite diagrams (quadrilateral + triangle); algebraic angles; "prove/name the quadrilateral" from partial angle information; rhombus diagonal-angle reasoning.
- Distractors: assuming all quadrilaterals have equal opposite angles; mixing rectangle/rhombus properties.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** when information is encoded by the figure: labelled-vertex naming, drawing/diagonal tasks, connected shapes, or angle questions whose layout, arcs, parallel marks, or side-tick marks supply conditions. The diagram must be constraint-consistent; state “not drawn to scale” when appropriate rather than allowing appearance to reveal an answer.
- **OPTIONAL** for questions whose complete data are stated in words or symbols. Include text-only and table-based items such as property descriptions, sides–vertices–diagonals tables, and symbolic angle-sum equations so the question bank is not entirely visual.
- **RECOMMENDED** for classification/property checklists. Every diagram should have a text-equivalent internal model containing the cyclic vertex order, edges, diagonals, labels, and marked constraints.

## Generator notes
- Ensure generated angle sets are consistent (triangle sums exactly 180°, quadrilateral 360°); when generating, compute the unknown from chosen knowns.
- Side-tick marks and angle arcs must match the stated properties (isosceles ⇒ two ticks).
- Generate polygon names from a stored cyclic vertex order. Accept rotations of that order and its reversal (for example, `ABCDE`, `BCDEA`, and `AEDCB`), but reject sequences that skip between non-adjacent vertices.
- For an n-gon, validate n ≥ 3, vertices = sides = n, diagonals from one vertex = n−3, and total diagonals = n(n−3)/2. When solving backwards from a diagonal count, reject instances without a unique valid integer n in the stated range.
- Treat convex/concave and general regular/irregular classification as supporting/enrichment metadata, not assumed examinable core content. Shape-specific side and angle properties in 9.2 and 9.3 remain core.
- Maintain a deliberate representation mix: use diagrams only where spatial relationships carry information, and use prose, symbolic, or table formats when they fully specify the mathematics.
- Possible item pattern: find the value of `x + y` in a triangle with one exterior angle.
- Cross-link: Ch8 parallel lines, Ch10 perimeter/area, Form 2 Ch4 full polygon theory (interior/exterior angle sums of n-gons).
