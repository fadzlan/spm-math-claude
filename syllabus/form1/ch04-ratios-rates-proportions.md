# Form 1, Chapter 4: Ratios, Rates and Proportions

## Overview
Introduces ratio, rate and proportion as related ways of comparing quantities, and connects them explicitly to fractions, decimals and percentages. Covers two- and three-quantity ratios, equivalent ratios, a broad range of rates, speed, proportions, percentage by proportion, and sharing in a given ratio. Named algebraic direct proportion is enrichment rather than core Form 1 content.

## Prerequisites
Fractions and division (Ch1); unit conversions (cm↔m, g↔kg, sen↔RM).

## Topic & subtopic breakdown

| # | Subtopic | Content coverage | Key skills / formulae | Typical question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Ratios | Ratio of two quantities `a:b`; ratio of three quantities `a:b:c`; equivalent ratios; simplest form; ratios with different units (convert first); relationship between ratio and fraction. | `a:b = ka:kb` for `k != 0`; `a:b` corresponds to `a/b` when comparing `a` with `b`; unit conversion before simplification. | Simplify or form a ratio; interpret a ratio as a fraction of a comparison quantity or of a total; find a ratio from data; express a ratio in the form `1:n` or `n:1`. |
| 4.2 | Rates | Rate as comparison of quantities measured in different units; common rates including unit price, wages, exchange rate, flow rate, speed, acceleration, pressure and density; average speed; unit conversion. | Rate = quantity / corresponding quantity; speed = distance / time; average speed = total distance / total time; use the unitary method and retain compound units. | Find, interpret or compare rates; best-value problems; calculate distance or time; identify the numerator and denominator quantities and state the rate unit. |
| 4.3 | Proportions, fractions, decimals and percentages | Proportion as equality of two ratios; solve an unknown in `a:b = c:d`; relationship among ratio, rate, proportion, fraction, decimal and percentage; determine a percentage by setting up a proportion; divide a quantity in a given ratio; daily-life problems such as recipes and mixtures. | `a/b = c/d` implies `ad = bc` when denominators are non-zero; `part/whole = percentage/100`; fraction-decimal-percentage conversion; share = `(ratio part / total ratio parts) x quantity`. | Find a missing term; express a comparison in multiple representations; find a percentage using proportion; divide an amount in ratio `p:q(:r)`; scale a recipe; solve ratio-and-proportion problems. |

### Scope boundary

- **Core**: numerical ratios, rates and proportions; fraction-decimal-percentage connections; percentage via proportion; the unitary method; sharing and contextual applications.
- **Enrichment**: naming or formalising direct proportion as `y = kx`, finding a constant of proportionality, and multi-object catch-up or meeting problems. These must carry `scope: enrichment` and must not be used merely to make a core Form 1 item harder.

## Difficulty strategies

### 4.1 Ratios
- **Easy**: two quantities, same units, small integers (8:12 → 2:3).
- **Medium**: different units requiring conversion (50 sen : RM2); three-quantity ratios; express as 1:n; connect `2:5` to the fraction `2/5` for the comparison stated.
- **Hard**: combine two ratios through a common term (a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5 ⇒ a:b:c = 8:12:15 — requires LCM alignment of the shared term); change-in-ratio problems ("if 3 is added to both terms…").
- Distractors: forgetting unit conversion; wrong order of terms; combining ratios without aligning the common term.

### 4.2 Rates
- **Easy**: direct division with an explicit compound unit, such as RM12 for 3 kg or 240 km in 4 h.
- **Medium**: mixed time or measurement units (2 h 30 min); best-buy comparisons; per-unit pricing; calculate a density, pressure, flow rate or acceleration from a supplied numerical relationship and units.
- **Hard**: two-stage journeys with average speed (total distance ÷ total time, not the arithmetic mean of speeds); compare rates after non-uniform unit conversions such as m/min to km/h; multi-step contexts involving two different rates.
- **Enrichment only**: catch-up and meeting problems involving relative motion.
- Distractors: taking the arithmetic mean of two speeds; reversing the rate; ignoring a unit mismatch; dropping or inventing a compound unit.

### 4.3 Proportions
- **Easy**: find `x` in `x:4 = 3:12`; convert a familiar fraction to a decimal and percentage.
- **Medium**: determine a percentage through `part/whole = x/100`; divide a quantity (RM840) in ratio 3:4 or 2:3:5; scale a recipe for non-integer servings.
- **Hard**: two-step problems (divide, then find a difference, percentage or fraction of a share); before/after ratio-change problems; unitary-method problems with a representation change; problems where the unknown is a difference (`a - b`) rather than a ratio term.
- **Enrichment only**: questions that introduce the notation `y = kx`, require a constant of proportionality, or explicitly assess "direct proportion" as an algebraic relation.
- Distractors: sharing in reversed order; using the ratio part without dividing by the total number of parts; confusing `a:b` with `b:a`; using `part/whole = 100/x`; treating a percentage as a whole number rather than a fraction over 100.

## Visual aids
- 4.1: **OPTIONAL** — bar model / tape diagram for three-part ratios; helpful for teaching-style questions.
- 4.2: **OPTIONAL** — simple journey sketch (two towns, arrow) for speed problems; not standard in SPM papers.
- 4.3: **RECOMMENDED** for sharing problems — a bar model split into p+q(+r) parts clarifies the question for medium/hard items.

## Generator notes
- Always state units; enforce conversion traps (sen/RM, g/kg, min/h) at Medium+.
- Sample across rate families rather than allowing speed to dominate. For acceleration, pressure and density items, provide the relevant quantities or relationship needed so the task assesses rate reasoning, not recall of later science formulae.
- When moving among ratio, fraction, decimal and percentage, define the comparison clearly. For example, ratio `a:b` gives `a/b` as the fraction of `b`, whereas the fraction of the combined total is `a/(a+b)`.
- For percentage-by-proportion items, generate consistent values from an internal tuple `(part, whole, percentage)` and verify `part/whole = percentage/100`; require `whole != 0` and specify any rounding rule.
- For best-value comparisons, choose prices so one option is clearly better after unitary division but not by inspection.
- Ratio-sharing answers should be integers at Easy/Medium; allow decimal ringgit at Hard.
- For average-speed items, derive every leg from a shared journey model and verify `total distance / total time`; never average the leg speeds unless the leg times are equal and that equivalence is intentional.
- Preserve the three-ratio alignment generator by choosing a common middle term through an LCM, then independently verify all three pairwise ratios after simplification.
- Add explicit `scope: enrichment` metadata to named direct-proportion algebra and catch-up/meeting templates.
- Cross-link: speed reappears in Form 2 Ch9 (with acceleration) and Form 4 Ch7 (graphs of motion); ratio skills are essential for Form 3 Ch4 scale drawings and Form 5 Ch1 variation.
