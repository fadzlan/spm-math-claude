# Form 1, Chapter 10: Perimeter and Area

## Overview
Measurement geometry: perimeters of shapes with straight and curved boundaries, and areas of squares, rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, kites and trapeziums, including composite figures and problems. Students measure and estimate lengths, perimeters and areas, then evaluate the accuracy of their estimates. Heavy real-life application (cost of fencing, carpet, land). Foundation for Form 2 Ch5/6 (circles, 3D shapes) and scale drawings.

## Prerequisites
Ch1 decimals/fractions; Ch9 shapes; unit conversion (mm–cm–m–km, area units cm²/m²).

## Difficulty strategies

### 10.1 Perimeter
- **Easy**: rectangle/square with integer sides.
- **Medium**: composite rectilinear shape (all sides given or easily deduced); decimal sides; estimate and then measure the perimeter of an irregular polygon on a controlled grid.
- **Hard**: missing side must be deduced by subtraction of opposite sides; algebraic sides (link Ch5); perimeter given → find a side; mixed units; a boundary containing a curve whose length is supplied or can be measured from an accurately scaled representation.
- **Estimation/accuracy**: require an estimate before measurement or calculation, then compare it with the reference value using a stated criterion such as the absolute difference. Do not use a digital or printed ruler task unless the intended display/print scale is controlled.

### 10.2 Areas
- **Easy**: triangle/parallelogram/kite/trapezium with the required dimensions given directly as integers.
- **Medium**: height shown perpendicular outside the shape (obtuse triangle trap); kite diagonals given; decimals; reverse (area given, find height or a kite diagonal); estimate an area from a grid and compare with a counted or calculated value.
- **Hard**: two-step (find area then cost at RM x per m²); unit conversion mid-problem (cm ↔ m); compare two designs; trapezium with one parallel side unknown; a kite embedded in a composite figure.
- Distractors: using a slant side as the height; confusing a kite's side lengths with its diagonals; forgetting ½ for a triangle, kite or trapezium; mixing cm and m.

### 10.3 P vs A relationship
- **Easy**: compute both for two given rectangles and compare.
- **Medium**: fixed perimeter, generate candidate rectangles, compare areas.
- **Hard**: same-area different-perimeter comparisons; make and test a conjecture across a clearly stated finite family, such as rectangles with positive integer side lengths and a fixed perimeter.
- Finite enumeration supports a conclusion only for the enumerated family; never label it a proof that a square maximises area among all rectangles of a fixed perimeter. A general maximisation proof is extension material and must use a valid general argument, not a table of selected examples.

### 10.4 Composite figures
- **Easy**: L-shape split into two rectangles.
- **Medium**: shaded region (rectangle minus triangle); composite with a trapezium or kite.
- **Hard**: multi-part figure with a dimension to deduce; shaded strip or polygonal cut-out problems; combinations involving a kite; cost-and-quantity chains (area → tins of paint given coverage per tin → number of tins → cost).
- Distractors: double-counting overlap; wrong decomposition; using outer dimensions only.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** for 10.1–10.4 questions in which the geometry of a shape supplies information — labelled SVG figures with dimensions; composite/shaded figures must be drawn (shading for shaded-region questions).
- **REQUIRED** controlled scale and intended physical/display dimensions for any question that asks the student to measure a length or curved boundary from the figure.
- **OPTIONAL** grid background for counting-style Easy questions and area/perimeter estimation.

## Generator notes
- Guarantee consistency: deducible missing sides must equal the difference of the corresponding opposite total lengths.
- Include kites in direct-area, reverse-dimension and composite-area generation. Validate that the labelled segments used in A = ½d₁d₂ are the two diagonals, not sides or partial diagonals.
- Curved-boundary perimeter is core as a measuring/estimating activity. Supply the curved length or provide a controlled-scale method of measuring it; do not require circumference, arc-length or sector formulae.
- Keep core items π-free. Circles, semicircles, quadrants, annuli ("shaded rings") and formula-based circular composites belong to Form 2 Ch5 or must be explicitly tagged `cross-chapter`, not generated as core Form 1 Chapter 10 items.
- For estimation items, store the reference measured/calculated value and the accepted accuracy rule. Accept reasonable estimates within the stated tolerance rather than requiring one unstated exact estimate.
- Cost problems: use RM per m², paint coverage m²/litre, tile counts per m².
- Cross-link: Ch9 shapes; Form 2 Ch5 circles (adds π sectors to composite figures); Form 3 Ch4 scale drawings applies area scaling.
