# Form 1, Chapter 3: Squares, Square Roots, Cubes and Cube Roots

## Overview
Introduces powers (squares and cubes) and their inverse operations. Establishes notation (n², n³, √, ∛), estimation strategies, and computation including with fractions and decimals. Foundation for Pythagoras (Ch13), area/volume work, and Form 3 indices.

## Prerequisites
Integer/decimal/fraction operations (Ch1); basic multiplication tables.

## Difficulty strategies

### 3.1
- **Easy**: perfect squares ≤ 144; single operation (evaluate 11² or the principal radical √81); identify both square roots in a statement such as x²=81.
- **Medium**: squares up to 20² = 400; square roots of decimals (0.49, 1.44) and fractions (25/49); multiplication that develops the generalisations √a×√a=a and √a×√b=√(ab); two-term expressions using any of +, −, × or ÷.
- **Hard**: estimate a non-integer square or locate a square root between consecutive integers ("√57 lies between which two integers?"); roots of mixed numbers (convert 2¼ first); combined expressions using the four operations (e.g. 3² × √144 ÷ 6 − 2); area→side and side→area applications; Pythagoras-flavoured two-square sums that remain within mastered Form 1 content.
- Distractors: √16=8 (halving); √(a+b)=√a+√b error; giving only 9 when solving x²=81; writing √81=±9 instead of distinguishing the principal radical from the equation's two roots; 0.5²=2.5.

### 3.2
- **Easy**: cubes of 1–10; ∛ of small perfect cubes; identify the single real root of x³=a.
- **Medium**: cubes of 11–15; cube roots of 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000; negative cubes ((−4)³=−64) and negative cube roots (∛(−27)=−3); two-term expressions using any of +, −, × or ÷.
- **Hard**: estimate a non-integer cube or locate a cube root between consecutive integers (for example, 4<∛70<5); cube roots of fractions/decimals (∛(27/64), ∛0.125); combined expressions involving squares, square roots, cubes and cube roots with the four operations; reverse problems (volume 512 cm³ → edge).
- Distractors: 3³ = 9; ∛8 = 2 confusion with √8; treating ∛0.008 as 0.2 vs 0.02.

## Visual aids
- **OPTIONAL** throughout: small square/cube sketch when the question involves area of a square or volume of a cube (helps realism for application questions). Pure computation: **NONE**.

## Generator notes
- In notation such as √81, the radical sign denotes the principal, non-negative value 9. In an equation such as x²=81, require both solutions x=±9. Do not attach ± directly to the meaning of the radical sign.
- Restrict exact-evaluation radicands to perfect powers (or simple fractions/decimals made from perfect powers). Unsimplified surds are outside core scope here and must not be used merely to make a hard answer; use them only if an item is explicitly tagged and justified as enrichment.
- Estimation questions cover squares, square roots, cubes and cube roots. Set bounds using consecutive benchmark squares or cubes, state the required accuracy where a decimal estimate is requested, and accept interval answers such as "between 7 and 8" when that is what is asked.
- Include +, −, × and ÷ across the generated item set, and include valid combined-operation items. Apply the normal order of operations, avoid division by zero, and generate operands backward so intermediate and final values remain appropriate for Form 1.
- For square-root multiplication, include examples that support both required generalisations: same radicand (√a×√a=a) and different radicands (√a×√b=√(ab), a,b≥0). Keep exact core questions within perfect-power products rather than requiring surd simplification.
- Square/cube area-volume links: side s → area s², edge e → volume e³. Good source of contextual questions.
- Cross-link: leads directly to Ch13 Pythagoras (c² = a² + b²) and Form 3 indices laws.
