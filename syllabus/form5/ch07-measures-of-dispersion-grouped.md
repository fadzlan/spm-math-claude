# Form 5, Chapter 7: Measures of Dispersion of Grouped Data

## Overview
Measures of dispersion for grouped data: equal class intervals, frequency tables, histograms, frequency polygons, ogives (cumulative frequency), grouped box plots, quartiles, interquartile range, variance, standard deviation, and interpretation/comparison of distributions. Estimated mean and modal class are retained as supporting prior knowledge rather than the focus of the chapter. The chapter also includes a statistical mini-project covering the complete investigation process.

## Prerequisites
Form 4 Ch8 (ungrouped dispersion); Form 2 Ch12 (grouped frequency tables, estimated mean and modal class); Form 1 Ch12 (statistical questions, data collection and representations).

## Difficulty strategies

### 7.1
- **Easy**: complete midpoints/boundaries; calculate a grouped variance or standard deviation from supplied totals.
- **Medium**: calculate variance/standard deviation using a structured table; combine an estimated mean (supporting knowledge) with standard deviation to compare distributions.
- **Hard**: compute standard deviation fully; compare two distributions and interpret consistency in context (e.g. which machine is more consistent); find a missing frequency from a supplied measure and then evaluate dispersion; investigate the effect of changing a class frequency.
- Distractors: using class limits instead of midpoints; variance formula sign/order errors (σ² = Σfx²/Σf − x̄², not the reverse); forgetting to take the square root; mean treated as exact rather than estimate.

### 7.2 Ogives and box plots
- **Easy**: complete cumulative frequency column; read median position.
- **Medium**: draw an ogive from a table (points at upper boundaries, starting at the lower boundary of the first class with cumulative frequency 0); estimate median/Q₁/Q₃/IQR; construct a grouped box plot from those estimates and the extreme values supplied or justified by the data.
- **Hard**: percentile reads (P₃₀, P₇₅-style); "number of students scoring more than k" (n − cf(k)); reverse (how many passed if pass mark chosen so that 60% pass — find mark from ogive); compare linked ogives and box plots; construct the original frequency table from a given ogive (differences of cumulative frequencies at boundaries); justify a comparison using median, IQR and distribution shape.
- Distractors: plotting at midpoints instead of upper boundaries; reading frequency instead of value; IQR = Q₃ − Q₁ with values read at wrong cf levels (n vs n+1 conventions — fix n/2 convention).

### 7.3 Histograms & polygons
- **Easy**: complete a partially drawn histogram.
- **Medium**: draw histogram with correct boundaries and heights; frequency polygon overlay.
- **Hard**: two frequency polygons on same axes with comparison commentary; histogram from table with a missing frequency derived from total; convert between histogram and table.
- Distractors: bars with gaps; using class limits instead of boundaries; plotting polygon vertices at boundaries instead of midpoints; applying an endpoint convention different from the one stated in the item.

### 7.4 Statistical mini-project
- **Easy**: identify a suitable statistical question, variable, population and simple collection method from a familiar context.
- **Medium**: organise a provided or collected data set, choose a suitable graph and measures, and describe what the results show.
- **Hard**: plan and carry out the full investigation cycle; justify methodological choices; connect the conclusion explicitly to the statistical question; discuss sampling or measurement limitations and propose a defensible improvement.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** — the full chart toolkit: histograms for equal class intervals (contiguous bars, boundary-labelled axis), frequency polygons (overlaid pairs in different styles), ogives (cumulative curves on a grid with reading guides), and grouped box plots linked to quartile estimates from ogives. All need labelled axes and appropriate scales; graphs that support reading estimates need suitable grids or guides. Tables (frequency/cumulative) are required as the shared data source.
- Generate charts from the same internal table to guarantee consistency.

## Generator notes
- Choose class intervals with uniform width, boundaries offset by 0.5 (e.g. 30–39 → 29.5–39.5); n ∈ {30, 40, 50, 60, 80} so quartile positions are clean (n/4, n/2, 3n/4 integers).
- Do not generate unequal-class-width or frequency-density tasks as core Form 5 Chapter 7 items. If retained for explicit enrichment, label them out of scope for core KSSM and do not mix them into ordinary difficulty progression.
- Mean/σ computations: precompute Σfx, Σfx² internally; at Hard give these partially and require completion.
- Missing-frequency items: pick p first, compute the mean, present mean and solve back (equation linear in p).
- Ogive reads: state "estimate" and accept tolerance (±1 unit) in answers; draw from exact cf data.
- Derive grouped box-plot quartiles from the same ogive model and use the stated or data-supported minimum and maximum. Store the five-number summary internally, apply the same reading tolerance as the ogive, and check that minimum ≤ Q₁ ≤ median ≤ Q₃ ≤ maximum.
- For a frequency polygon, always plot class-midpoint vertices. Join the ends to zero only when required by the selected textbook/examination convention and when adjacent-class midpoint positions are available or can be constructed consistently; otherwise stop at the first and last plotted class midpoint.
- Mini-project generation must retain an auditable chain from statistical question → collection plan and data → grouped table → selected graph/measures → analysis → contextual conclusion and justification. Reject projects whose conclusion is not supported by the generated data or whose sample cannot answer the stated question.
