# Form 5, Chapter 1: Variation

## Overview
Variation (proportionality): direct, inverse, joint and combined variation. Students explore relationships using equations, tables and graphs; translate statements such as “`y` varies directly as `x²`” into `y = kx²`; connect the constant of variation to a constant ratio and the gradient of an appropriate graph; find `k`; and calculate unknowns. The prescribed forms include selected positive integer powers and fractional powers expressed as roots, subject to their real-number domains. Contexts are mathematical models whose assumptions must be stated.

## Prerequisites
Form 2 Ch3 (formulae, change of subject); Form 3 Ch1 (indices); Form 1 Ch4 (ratios/proportion).

## Difficulty strategies

### 1.1 Direct
- **Easy**: y ∝ x, integer k; find k then one value.
- **Medium**: `y ∝ x²` or `y ∝ √x`; complete a table with two gaps, including one that requires solving for `x`; verify the constant ratio.
- **Hard**: determine the prescribed power by testing ratios; obtain `k` as the gradient of a supplied or constructed `y`-against-`xⁿ` graph; or use a context where the varying quantity must first be expressed in terms of the stated variable.

### 1.2 Inverse
- **Easy**: y ∝ 1/x basics.
- **Medium**: `y ∝ 1/x²`; tables with gaps; test the constant product; work-rate problems with explicit fixed conditions.
- **Hard**: distinguish inverse and inverse-square relationships by testing products; obtain `k` as the gradient of a `y`-against-`1/xⁿ` graph; compare multiplicative changes; or determine a prescribed small integer/root power from exact data without logarithms.

### 1.3 Joint/combined
- **Easy**: z ∝ xy, find k and one value.
- **Medium**: z ∝ x²/y; three-variable tables with one gap per row.
- **Hard**: two-stage problems in which stated conditions change; find one variable from a relation involving a prescribed root or power; use one complete row to determine `k` before applying it elsewhere; or analyse multiplicative changes in a combined relation.
- Distractors: forgetting to square/cube when substituting; using k from a different relationship row; inverse applied as direct; in percentage-change questions, adding percentages instead of multiplying factors; wrong root/power order in change of subject.

## Visual aids
- **CORE/RECOMMENDED** — use exact-value tables and graphs to explore and determine relationships, not merely to recognise a graph shape. Direct-variation tasks should support a straight-line graph of `y` against `xⁿ`; inverse-variation tasks should support a straight-line graph of `y` against `1/xⁿ`. The line passes through the origin and has gradient `k` under the stated model.
- Store plotted coordinates and the transformed horizontal-axis values as exact semantic data. Generate graph and table from the same underlying relation; verify scales, labels, origin, gradient and every populated cell.
- Context diagrams, such as a cylinder for a relation involving `r²h`, are **OPTIONAL** and must show only information used by the model.

## Generator notes
- A useful structured template is: “It is given that `y` varies directly as the square of `x` and inversely as `z`...” followed by finding the equation and values under new stated conditions. Do not label any template “guaranteed” or infer paper frequency without a dated assessment corpus.
- Keep powers within the prescribed forms: positive integer powers and fractional powers represented as `x^(1/n)`, where `n` is a positive integer. Do not introduce arbitrary real, negative or logarithmically determined exponents as core content.
- Enforce domains before sampling or solving. For `x^(1/n)`, require `x >= 0` when `n` is even (and use the principal real root); for `1/xⁿ`, require `x != 0`; for `1/x^(1/n)`, additionally exclude values that make the real root undefined and always exclude `x = 0`. Reject any item whose requested root gives inadmissible or multiple context-dependent values.
- Choose data so `k` is a small integer or simple fraction and all displayed table/graph values are exact. A hard item may require a root only when the selected solution is unique under the stated domain.
- For table completion, include at least one complete data pair/row from which `k` can be found and ensure each missing value is uniquely solvable.
- Include core tasks that ask students to establish a constant ratio/product from a table and to connect it with graph gradient: `k = y/xⁿ` for a plot of `y` against `xⁿ`, or `k = yxⁿ` for a plot of `y` against `1/xⁿ`.
- State modelling assumptions in physical or work-rate contexts. For example, an inverse workers-time model assumes equal constant work rates, fixed workload and unchanged working hours/conditions; an inverse-square field model holds source strength and other variables constant; a gas-style pressure-volume-temperature relation fixes the amount of gas and treats the simplified relation as valid only under the stated conditions.
- Do not imply that a fitted variation relation is an exact universal physical law. Phrase conclusions as consequences of the given model and range of conditions, and require “all other relevant quantities remain constant” when only selected variables change.
- Independently recompute `k`, all table cells, plotted coordinates, gradients and requested values. Reject items with inconsistent data, invalid domains, zero denominators, ambiguous roots, non-unique answers or assumptions contradicted by the context.
