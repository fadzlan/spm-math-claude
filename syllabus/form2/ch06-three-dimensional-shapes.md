# Form 2, Chapter 6: Three-Dimensional Geometrical Shapes

## Overview
Properties and classification of three-dimensional shapes; nets of pyramids, prisms, cylinders and cones; and surface area and volume of right prisms, right cylinders, right pyramids, right cones and spheres, including composite solids and applications.

Unless explicitly tagged otherwise, material in this file has `scope: core`. Euler's relation, enumeration of the 11 cube nets, systematic cross-sections, displacement, optimisation and capsule solids have `scope: enrichment` and must not be generated as ordinary Form 2 assessment content.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch10 (area); Form 2 Ch5 (circle circumference and area); Form 1 Ch13 (Pythagoras, when a slant height must be found).

## Curriculum scope boundary

- **Classification:** both right and oblique three-dimensional shapes may be shown and classified. Learners may describe their geometric properties and distinguish right from oblique examples.
- **Derivation and calculation:** derive and use surface-area and volume formulae only for **right solids**. Do not ask learners to calculate the surface area or volume of an oblique prism, cylinder, pyramid or cone as Form 2 core content.
- Difficulty does not change this boundary. An oblique calculation or any topic tagged `enrichment` remains out of core even in a Hard item.

## Difficulty strategies

### 6.1 Properties and classification
- **Easy**: name a solid from a picture or short property list; count vertices of a familiar prism or pyramid.
- **Medium**: compare a right solid with an oblique example; classify an unfamiliar prism or pyramid from its base and side faces.
- **Hard**: use several necessary properties to select or justify a classification. Keep calculations on right solids only.

### 6.2 Nets
- **Easy**: match a clearly labelled prism, pyramid, cylinder or cone to its net.
- **Medium**: complete a missing face; transfer dimensions between a solid and its net; reject a net with mismatched joined edges.
- **Hard**: infer dimensions and calculate area from a valid net; validate a cone sector using both slant height and arc length. Enumeration of all 11 cube nets is enrichment, not a Hard core strategy.

### 6.3 Surface area
- **Easy**: right cuboid or cylinder with integer dimensions; curved surface area when `r` and `h` are supplied.
- **Medium**: right triangular prism; right cone with slant height supplied; right pyramid or hemisphere; one reverse calculation.
- **Hard**: composite right solids with exposed surfaces clearly specified; surface area to an unknown dimension; multi-rate material-cost contexts. Formal minimisation is enrichment.
- Distractors: including joined or hidden faces; using perpendicular height instead of slant height for a cone; forgetting a base; mixing units.

### 6.4 Volume
- **Easy**: right cuboid, prism or cylinder with clean arithmetic.
- **Medium**: right pyramid or cone requiring the `⅓` factor; reverse problems; capacity conversion.
- **Hard**: a two- or three-component right composite; pouring between containers; melting/recasting; flow-rate filling; percentage-filled tanks. Do not use capsule solids or displacement unless the item is tagged enrichment.
- Distractors: forgetting `⅓`; confusing radius and diameter; using slant height as perpendicular height; incorrect capacity conversion; rounding intermediate values.

## Net validation rules

- Build the net and the answer from the same solid model. Every face must occur exactly once, shared edge lengths must agree, and folding must not force faces to overlap.
- A right prism net has two congruent parallel polygonal bases and one rectangular lateral face for each base edge. A right pyramid net has one polygonal base and one triangular face for each base edge.
- A right cylinder net consists of two circles of radius `r` and a rectangle whose sides are `h` and `2πr`.
- A right cone net consists of a base circle of radius `r` and a sector of radius equal to the slant height `l`. For a non-degenerate cone, enforce `l > r`, sector arc length `2πr`, and sector angle
  `θ = 360°r/l`, with `0° < θ < 360°`. If perpendicular height `h` is also present, verify `l² = r² + h²`.
- Reject a generated net if labels become ambiguous after folding, dimensions contradict the solid, the sector angle is invalid, or an alleged distractor is also foldable.

## Visual aids
- Solid-identification and mensuration items normally need a clear isometric or oblique **drawing** with dimension labels; an oblique drawing style does not mean the mathematical solid itself is oblique.
- Mark right angles or state “right prism”, “right pyramid”, “right cylinder” or “right cone” where the classification could otherwise be uncertain. A slanted-looking rendered edge must not accidentally change the intended solid.
- Nets questions require a 2D net diagram. Cone nets must visibly use a sector, not a triangle, for the curved surface.
- Composite solids require a diagram and an explicit indication of joined, exposed and hidden surfaces.
- Cross-section diagrams are required only for enrichment cross-section items.

## Generator notes
- Required metadata: `scope: core|enrichment`, `solid_family`, `right_or_oblique`, `task_type: classify|net|surface_area|volume`, `calculator`, `pi_value`, units and exposed-surface specification where relevant.
- For `task_type: surface_area|volume` with `scope: core`, enforce `right_or_oblique: right` (or `not_applicable` for a sphere). Oblique solids are permitted in core only when `task_type: classify`.
- Keep π instructions explicit (`22/7`, `3.142`, calculator value or exact in terms of `π`) and apply the chosen convention consistently. Round only at the end unless the question states otherwise.
- Generate the solid, dimensions and exposed-face model first; independently recompute the keyed answer before rendering the question and diagram.
- Slant height and perpendicular height must be labelled distinctly. If `h` is given for a right cone and `l` must be found, use `l² = r² + h²` and ensure the prerequisite is intended.
- Composite core items may use two components at Medium and up to three at Hard, but all components involved in mensuration must be right solids (or portions of spheres). State which surfaces are joined or exposed.
- Pouring and recasting questions must conserve volume exactly. Displacement is a different, enrichment-only context.
