# Form 2, Chapter 7: Coordinates

## Overview
The core content is distance between two points, midpoint, and geometric problems on the coordinate plane (shapes, area). Plotting and reading points in all four quadrants is supporting prerequisite knowledge. This chapter is a foundation for graphs of functions (Ch8), straight lines (Form 3 Ch9) and Form 4 work.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch1 (integers, number line); plotting and reading Cartesian coordinates, including axes, origin and quadrants.

## Difficulty strategies

### 7.1
- **Scope**: Supporting prerequisite practice; do not treat plotting/quadrant identification as the chapter's main content standard.
- **Easy**: read/plot points in quadrant I.
- **Medium**: all four quadrants; points with negative coordinates; identify quadrant without plotting.
- **Hard**: points on axes vs quadrants distinction; pattern of points (collinear points — find missing coordinate).

### 7.2 Distance
- **Easy**: horizontal/vertical segments (Δx or Δy = 0).
- **Medium**: general points giving Pythagorean-triple distances (3-4-5 scaled).
- **Hard**: non-integer distances with rounding; perimeter of triangle/quadrilateral from coordinates (3 distances chained); verify collinearity using equal gradients (with vertical-line handling) or the zero-area determinant test. As an **extension**, find an unknown coordinate of a point given distance (for example, solve (x − 2)² + 9 = 25 and account for both solutions).
- Distractors: sign errors with negatives (x₂ − x₁ where both negative); forgetting to square root; averaging instead of differencing.

### 7.3 Midpoint
- **Easy**: positive integer coordinates, integer midpoint.
- **Medium**: mixed signs; midpoint with halves.
- **Hard**: find endpoint B given M and A (requires doubling/subtracting — classic reverse); combined with distance (find distance from midpoint to vertex). Treat midpoint as the centre of a circle from a diameter as an **extension**.
- Distractors: halving the difference instead of the sum; subtracting when finding endpoint instead of using B = 2M − A.

## Visual aids
- **RECOMMENDED** — Cartesian grid SVG with plotted and labelled points for most question types at Easy/Medium; Hard reverse problems can be text-only.
- **REQUIRED** when the question references a drawn shape or asks to read coordinates.

## Generator notes
- Use integer coordinates within −10…10 to fit standard grids.
- For clean distances, choose coordinate differences from Pythagorean triples.
- Endpoint-from-midpoint questions: pick A and B first, compute M, then present A and M.
- For collinearity, prefer the determinant test `(x₂ − x₁)(y₃ − y₁) − (y₂ − y₁)(x₃ − x₁) = 0`, which also handles vertical lines. If using `AB + BC = AC`, first establish that B is between A and C; otherwise choose the distance equality that corresponds to the actual middle point.
- For extension questions with an unknown coordinate and a fixed distance, solve and validate every candidate; state an additional restriction if a unique answer is required.
- Cross-link: Ch8 graphs of functions (plotting); Ch10 gradient; Form 1 Ch13 Pythagoras; Form 3 Ch9 straight lines (distance/midpoint combined with gradient).
