# Form 2, Chapter 9: Speed and Acceleration

## Overview
Quantifies motion in a fixed direction: speed as a rate, average speed for multi-stage journeys, uniform and non-uniform speed, acceleration and deceleration as rate of change of speed, and unit conversions (km/h ↔ m/s, min ↔ h). Tables and simple graphs are used to develop and compare the meanings of uniform and non-uniform speed; full analysis of motion graphs is reserved for Form 4 Ch7.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch4 (rates); unit conversions of time and distance.

## Difficulty strategies

### 9.1 Speed
- **Easy**: single-stage v = s/t with clean numbers (150 km, 2 h).
- **Medium**: time in h:min (2 h 30 min); unit conversion (km/h ↔ m/s); find time and state arrival clock time; complete or compare tables showing distances travelled in equal time intervals; match a uniform/non-uniform motion description to a simple graph.
- **Hard (core)**: multi-stage average speed (60 km at 40 km/h then 60 km at 60 km/h — average is NOT 50); rest stops included in total time; round-trip with different speeds each way; express speed in two unit systems; explain, using a table or simple plotted graph, why motion is uniform or non-uniform.
- **Enrichment only**: catch-up, overtaking and relative-speed problems. Tag generated items `scope: enrichment`; do not use them as evidence of mastery of the Form 2 core standard.
- Distractors: averaging the stage speeds instead of using total distance ÷ total time; ignoring stop duration; time-unit mixing (minutes treated as hours); treating any increasing graph as uniform speed without checking equal distance changes over equal time intervals.

### 9.2 Acceleration
- **Easy**: a = (v − u)/t with u = 0.
- **Medium**: non-zero u; deceleration to rest (v = 0 → find t or distance-free questions); unit labelling.
- **Hard**: two-phase acceleration (accelerate, then decelerate — compute each phase); convert speeds to m/s before computing a in m/s²; find time to reach a speed from acceleration and initial speed; comparative reasoning (which car accelerates more given u, v, t data).
- Distractors: forgetting sign for deceleration; unit inconsistency (km/h per s); swapping u and v.
- Keep all core acceleration questions in a stated fixed direction. Do not generate turning, reversal of direction, circular motion, vector components, or acceleration caused solely by a direction change.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED for representation items** — use a distance–time table and/or a simple graph when the target is to distinguish or compare uniform and non-uniform speed. Axes must be labelled with quantities and units, plotted values must agree exactly with the source table, and equal time intervals should be easy to compare.
- Suitable core graph tasks are limited to constructing/reading plotted values and recognising that equal distance changes in equal time intervals indicate uniform speed, whereas varying distance changes indicate non-uniform speed.
- **Reserve for Form 4 Ch7**: formal distance–time or speed–time graph analysis, gradient calculations, area under a graph, interpretation of multiple motion segments, and deriving distance or acceleration from graph geometry.
- **OPTIONAL** — a simple journey diagram for stages with speeds/times labelled, or a table of stage data for average-speed calculations.

## Generator notes
- Choose stage distances/speeds so stage times are terminating decimals or simple fractions.
- For uniform-speed tables, use equal time intervals and a constant distance increment. For non-uniform-speed tables, vary at least one distance increment while keeping distances non-decreasing; make the intended contrast unambiguous.
- Build a graph and its table from the same internal data model. Validate axis labels, units, plotted coordinates and the uniform/non-uniform classification together.
- Do not infer instantaneous speed, acceleration, or distance travelled from the slope or area of a graph in this chapter.
- Catch-up/overtaking/relative-speed enrichment: set up so the meeting time is clean (relative speed divides distance gap) and retain the explicit `scope: enrichment` tag.
- For acceleration, state that the object continues along the same fixed direction and keep all speeds in compatible units before applying a = (v − u)/t.
- Malaysian contexts: PLUS highway, KTM, bas persiaran, cycling, LRT.
- Cross-link: Form 1 Ch4 rates; Form 4 Ch7 formalises distance–time and speed–time graph analysis.
