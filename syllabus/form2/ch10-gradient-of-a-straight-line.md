# Form 2, Chapter 10: Gradient of a Straight Line

## Overview
Introduces gradient (slope) of a straight line by deriving vertical change ÷ horizontal change from concrete situations and connecting concrete, graphical, and symbolic representations. Covers sign and magnitude meaning, computing gradient from two points or from a graph, the intercept formula, horizontal/vertical lines, and comparing steepness. Bridge between coordinates (Ch7) and straight-line equations (Form 3 Ch9).

## Prerequisites
Ch7 coordinates; directed numbers.

## Difficulty strategies

- **Easy**: explain vertical change and horizontal change in a simple ramp or graph; connect a rise/run triangle to `m = rise/run`; two points with positive gradient and integer `m`; horizontal/vertical lines (`m = 0` or undefined).
- **Medium**: move between a concrete description, graph, and symbolic calculation; negative gradients; fractional gradients; read gradient from a grid graph by counting squares; compare steepness of several lines using `|m|` while retaining the sign to describe direction.
- **Hard**: justify why the same straight line gives the same rise/run ratio using different point pairs; gradient involving points with both coordinates negative; gradient from nonzero intercepts (`m = −b/a`); rate-of-change contexts (tank filling graphs — gradient as a rate). Unknown-coordinate and parallel-line questions are permitted applications, but should not dominate or replace derivation and representation tasks.
- Distractors: inverting rise/run (`Δx/Δy`); sign errors with negative coordinates; treating a larger signed gradient as necessarily steeper; discarding the sign when direction is asked; undefined vs zero confusion.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** for graph-reading gradient questions — Cartesian grid SVG with the line drawn and a marked rise/run triangle is ideal for teaching-style items; lines must pass through clearly readable grid points.
- Two-point computation questions: **OPTIONAL**.

## Generator notes
- When generating a line to be read from a graph, pick two exact lattice points first, then draw.
- Include horizontal (m = 0) and vertical (undefined) cases regularly — classic exam traps.
- Build derivation items from a shared underlying line or rate model, then render the concrete description, graph, rise/run triangle, and symbolic calculation consistently. Do not reduce the chapter to repeated substitution in the two-point formula.
- For an x-intercept `a` and y-intercept `b`, use the points `(a, 0)` and `(0, b)` to derive `m = (b − 0)/(0 − a) = −b/a`. Require `a ≠ 0` and `b ≠ 0`, so the line has two distinct, nonzero axis intercepts. These questions also prepare students for Form 3 Ch9 (`x/a + y/b = 1`).
- Use signed gradient to describe direction: positive lines rise from left to right, negative lines fall, and zero-gradient lines are horizontal. Use `|m|` only to compare steepness; equal `|m|` does not mean equal direction or parallel lines.
- Treat equal gradients of parallel non-vertical lines and arbitrary unknown-coordinate problems as applications. Sample them less often than meaning, derivation, graph reading, and representation-conversion tasks. For vertical parallel lines, state that both gradients are undefined rather than applying an equality of numerical gradients.
- Cross-link: Ch7 coordinates; Ch8 graphs; Form 3 Ch9 straight lines (full equation work); Form 4 Ch7 gradient of speed-time graph = acceleration.
