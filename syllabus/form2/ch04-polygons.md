# Form 2, Chapter 4: Polygons

## Overview
Extends Form 1 Ch9: regular polygons, interior and exterior angles of n-sided polygons, angle sums, number of sides from angle information, and constructions of regular polygons. Combined with parallel lines and triangles, this chapter generates many SPM geometry items.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch8 (angles, parallel lines), Ch9 (basic polygons, triangles, quadrilaterals).

## Difficulty strategies

### 4.1
- **Easy**: exterior angle of regular hexagon (60°); interior of square.
- **Medium**: interior/exterior of n = 8, 9, 10, 12; find n given exterior 40° (n = 9); symmetry axes.
- **Hard**: given interior angle 156°, find n (exterior = 24°, n = 15); two-step problems (each interior angle is twice the exterior — find n).
- Distractors: using 180n instead of (n − 2)·180; confusing interior with exterior; non-divisible angle values (ensure generated regular polygons have clean angle arithmetic or expect fractions deliberately).

### 4.1 construction
- **Easy**: complete a partly drawn equilateral triangle, square, or regular hexagon using stated tools; select the next step from a diagram; state that the angle at the centre is 360°/n.
- **Medium**: construct a regular polygon by marking equal central angles on a circle, or by using a supported ruler-and-compasses construction; annotate the construction and explain why equal central angles give equal chord lengths.
- **Hard**: compare two construction methods, diagnose a construction that does not close, or write and justify a sequence of dynamic-geometry operations (for example, rotate a point repeatedly through 360°/n about the centre and join consecutive images).
- A construction prompt must name the permitted tools and the required precision or evidence (construction arcs, angle markings, measurements, or software operations). Do not silently treat a measured protractor construction as an exact straightedge-and-compasses construction.
- For **exact ruler-and-compasses** items, use only polygons for which the intended construction has been supplied and independently validated (safe elementary choices include n = 3, 4, 6, 8, and 12). Do not ask learners to prove the general constructibility of an n-gon, and do not generate arbitrary n from the angle-calculation list below.
- For **ruler-and-protractor** items, choose n for which 360°/n can be marked at the stated precision, require a circle/common centre, and allow an explicit drawing tolerance. For **dynamic geometry**, generate equal rotations or a regular-polygon command and require the learner to relate the operation to equal sides and equal angles.

### 4.2
- **Easy**: sum of interior angles of a heptagon (900°).
- **Medium**: irregular polygon with all angles but one given — find the missing angle; find n given sum (e.g. 1440° ⇒ n = 10).
- **Hard**: algebraic angles in a polygon (angles x, 2x, …, solve); problems mixing polygons with parallel lines (exterior angle + alternate angles); two polygons sharing a side; "exterior angles are in ratio" problems. Angle chasing at a point where polygon corners meet may be used only as an enrichment context, not as formal tessellation content.
- Distractors: forgetting exterior sum is always 360°; arithmetic slips with (n − 2) × 180°; treating irregular polygon angles as equal.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** — labelled polygon SVGs: regular polygons with marked angle, irregular polygons with known angles labelled and unknown marked; combined diagrams (polygon + parallel lines) must show parallel markers.
- Construction items require staged construction diagrams or an interactive/dynamic-geometry equivalent. Preserve construction arcs, the centre, radii, equal-angle/equal-length marks, and the permitted tool cues needed to justify the method.
- Angle-deduction diagrams may be drawn approximately to scale and should show angle arcs for known/unknown angles. Measurement/construction items must instead use a controlled printable scale or state the accepted tolerance; do not let an approximately scaled deduction diagram become a measurement question.

## Generator notes
- Generate unknown-angle questions backwards: choose all angles, compute the unknown by subtraction from the sum.
- Regular-polygon n: use n ∈ {5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, 24} for clean exterior angles (360/n).
- Keep the preceding list for **angle calculations only**. Construction generators must choose n according to the declared method and tool constraints, verify that all vertices lie on the intended circle, verify equal consecutive chord lengths/central angles within the stated tolerance, and check that the final side closes without a duplicate vertex.
- A rationale question must test geometry rather than mere recall of an ordered recipe: acceptable links include “radii locate vertices on one circle”, “equal central angles are 360°/n”, “equal central angles subtend equal chords”, and “a rotation about the centre preserves distance and angle”.
- Example item templates: "The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. Find the number of sides." and "The diagram shows a regular hexagon and a square sharing a side — find angle x."
- **Scope boundary**: formal tessellation is Form 5 Chapter 5. Tiling or polygons meeting at a point may appear here only as clearly tagged enrichment or as a local angle-at-a-point context; do not assess tessellation definitions, creation, or classification as Form 2 core.
- Cross-link: Form 1 Ch8/9; Form 5 Ch5 tessellation; Form 3 Ch6 circle theorems use polygon-in-circle angles.
