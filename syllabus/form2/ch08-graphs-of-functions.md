# Form 2, Chapter 8: Graphs of Functions

## Overview
Introduction to functions and their graphs: the concept of a function as a relationship, one-to-one and many-to-one functions, tables of values, and graphs in the family \(y=ax^n\), where \(n\in\{-2,-1,1,2,3\}\). Includes reading and interpreting graphs, using real-life graphs to describe trends and make predictions, and solving equations by finding intersections of two graphs. Leads to Form 3 Ch9 straight lines and Form 4 Ch1 quadratic functions.

## Prerequisites
Ch7 coordinates; Form 1 Ch5 substitution.

## Difficulty strategies

### 8.1 Functions
- **Easy**: one-step rules (y = 3x); find output.
- **Medium**: two-step rules; identify a function versus a non-function from ordered pairs (one input mapping to two outputs makes the relation a non-function); classify a valid function as one-to-one or many-to-one.
- **Hard**: find all possible inputs for a given output; compare the classifications of a function on two stated domains; justify separately (i) whether the relation is a function and (ii) whether that function is one-to-one or many-to-one.

### 8.2 Graphs
- **Easy**: complete a table for \(y=2x\) or \(y=x^2\), including negative and positive \(x\); plot and read a value.
- **Medium**: complete and compare tables for two members of \(y=ax^n\); read \(y\) at a fractional \(x\) from a drawn graph; distinguish the five exponent families, including \(y=a/x^2\) and \(y=a/x\).
- **Hard**: explore reciprocal and reciprocal-square behaviour near \(x=0\) without evaluating at zero; compare the effects of positive and negative \(a\); infer a prescribed-family equation from its shape and labelled points.
- Distractors: misplotting negative \(y\); treating \(x=0\) as permitted for a negative exponent; joining curved-graph points with straight segments; confusing \(y=a/x^2\) with \(y=a/x\); reading an \(x\)-value when asked for a \(y\)-value; claiming a reciprocal curve crosses an asymptote.

### 8.3 Interpreting and using graphs
- **Easy**: read and state a value or a clear increasing/decreasing/constant trend from a labelled real-life graph.
- **Medium**: compare rates of change qualitatively across intervals; interpolate a value and interpret it with units; read the coordinates of one intersection of two supplied graphs.
- **Hard**: make and qualify an extrapolated prediction; determine all intersections within a stated window and use them as approximate solutions of \(f(x)=g(x)\); explain why a displayed graph may not support a prediction outside its data range.
- Distractors: treating a graph as continuing indefinitely without contextual justification; omitting units; reporting only one of several intersections; giving the \(y\)-coordinate when the requested equation solution is the \(x\)-coordinate.

## Visual aids
- **REQUIRED** for graph-reading questions — SVG Cartesian grid with the drawn line/curve, axis labels and scales. For "draw the graph" questions provide the grid (blank or with table).
- 8.1 arrow diagrams: **RECOMMENDED** (two-column mapping SVG).
- Real-life graphs: **REQUIRED** to include labelled axes, units, scale, stated observation interval and enough contextual information to support the requested interpretation or prediction.
- Intersection questions: **REQUIRED** to show both graphs on the same axes, distinguish them by direct labels or an accessible legend, and use a scale that makes every intended intersection readable.

## Generator notes
- Choose nonzero \(a\) and input values so table values stay within −20…20 and grids of −10…10; do not include \(x=0\) for \(n=-1\) or \(n=-2\).
- For \(y=ax^2\), include \(x=0\) and symmetric positive/negative input pairs when the intended skill is recognising its shape or many-to-one nature. Do not generate translated general quadratics or require Form 4 turning-point/root methods as core Form 2 work.
- For read-off questions, generate the curve from the same function used for the table to guarantee accuracy; mark gridlines at unit intervals.
- For reciprocal families, draw separate branches and never connect them across \(x=0\). Keep plotted samples a safe distance from undefined values and viewport overflow.
- Generate intersection tasks from chosen intersection coordinates (or independently solve the equations first), then render both graphs; validate the number of visible intersections and the accepted read-off tolerance.
- For contextual prediction tasks, store the source data/domain and an expected interpretation. Reject prompts whose trend is ambiguous or whose prediction needs an unstated assumption.
