# Form 2, Chapter 13: Simple Probability

## Overview
Introduces probability of single events: experiment, outcome, sample space, event; probability as fraction/decimal/percentage between 0 and 1; equally likely outcomes; complement events. Leads to Form 4 Ch9 combined events.

## Prerequisites
Fractions (Form 1 Ch1); sets/counting (Form 1 Ch11 helpful).

## Topic & subtopic breakdown

| # | Subtopic | Content coverage | Key skills / formulae | Typical question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13.1 | Experimental probability vs theoretical probability | Experiment, outcome, sample space, event; carry out or simulate repeated trials; record frequencies; calculate experimental probability; observe that experimental probability tends towards theoretical probability as the number of trials increases; impossible (0) and certain (1) events. | P(experimental) = frequency of event/number of trials; P(theoretical) = n(E)/n(S) for equally likely outcomes. | Conduct a coin, die, spinner, or digital simulation; complete a frequency table; compute and compare experimental and theoretical probabilities; interpret variation between runs and the values 0 and 1. |
| 13.2 | Theoretical probability of equally likely outcomes | P(E) = n(E)/n(S) for single events: cards, dice, coins, spinners, balls in boxes, letters of words; probability as fraction in lowest terms / decimal / percentage; complement P(E′) = 1 − P(E); expected number of occurrences (n trials × P). | P(E) = n(E)/n(S); P(E′) = 1 − P(E). | Compute probability of single event; complement events; expected frequency; find unknown count given probability. |

## Difficulty strategies

### 13.1
- **Easy**: list the sample space of one toss, roll, spin, or draw; conduct a short experiment or simulation and record the frequency of an event.
- **Medium**: calculate experimental probability from a frequency table; compare it with the theoretical probability and explain why the values may differ; repeat a simulation with more trials and compare the results.
- **Hard**: analyse results from several trial sizes and describe the tendency towards theoretical probability as the number of trials increases; use a large experimental data set to estimate probability while recognising that an estimate need not equal the theoretical value exactly; identify impossible/certain events from descriptions.

### 13.2
- **Easy**: P(specific number on die); P(red ball) from a box (counts given).
- **Medium**: compound descriptions of a single event: P(even number), P(prime), P(letter is a vowel in "MATEMATIK"); complement questions (P(not red)); convert between fraction/decimal/percentage.
- **Hard**: find an unknown number of balls given P(blue) = 2/5 and counts of other colours (solve n/(n + k)); expected frequency over N repetitions of the same trial (P × N); probability from a table/chart of counts (data link); redefine a smaller sample space explicitly before asking for the probability of one selection from it (for example, "one student is chosen from the 18 students who passed").
- Distractors: not reducing to lowest terms when required; using the number of outcome types instead of the number of equally likely physical outcomes; counting the sample space wrongly for words with repeated letters (each letter card counts — "MATEMATIK" has 10 letters, M×2, A×3, T×2).

## Visual aids
- **RECOMMENDED** — spinner diagram (SVG circle divided into labelled sectors), box-of-balls illustration, card set icons. Sample-space questions on dice/coins: **OPTIONAL**.

## Generator notes
- Define exactly one trial and its sample space. For expected frequency, state that the same trial is reset and repeated under unchanged conditions (for example, a drawn card is returned before the next repetition).
- Do not generate multi-draw, dependent-event, conditional-probability, tree-diagram, or general without-replacement questions as core Form 2 items. A restricted group may be used only when it is explicitly defined as the sample space for one selection.
- For simulation items, specify a reproducible procedure or provide the simulated frequency data. Use several increasing trial counts when assessing the long-run tendency, and do not imply that experimental probability must equal theoretical probability after a fixed number of trials.
- Keep denominators ≤ 40 for Easy/Medium; simplify fractions.
- Expected-frequency answers should be integers; choose N as a multiple of the probability denominator.
- Letter-probability questions with repeated letters are a signature SPM item — include them.
- Cross-link: Form 1 Ch11 set counting; Form 4 Ch9 combined events (tree diagrams, with/without replacement).
