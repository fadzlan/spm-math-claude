# Form 2, Chapter 2: Factorisation and Algebraic Fractions

## Overview
This chapter develops expansion and factorisation, then uses both processes to perform addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and combined operations on algebraic expressions, including algebraic fractions. These are core Form 2 skills and underpin later work with algebraic formulae and quadratic expressions.

Unless a row or example is explicitly tagged otherwise, its scope is **core**. Material tagged **enrichment** must not be generated as ordinary Form 2 assessment content.

## Prerequisites
Form 1 Ch5 (algebraic expressions, like terms, and multiplication/division of algebraic terms); factors and multiples (Form 1 Ch2); squares (Form 1 Ch3).

## Difficulty strategies

### 2.1 Expansion
- **Easy**: one bracket with integer coefficients, such as `3(2x - 5)`; a product such as `(x + 2)(x + 3)` with positive terms.
- **Medium**: products such as `(2x - 3)(x + 4)`; squares such as `(x - 5)^2`; negative middle terms; expand two products and collect like terms.
- **Hard (within core scope)**: multi-product simplification such as `(3x - 2)^2 - (x + 1)(x - 4)`; rational numerical coefficients chosen to simplify cleanly; numerical use of identities such as `99^2` or `52^2 - 48^2`; geometric contexts with valid dimensions and stated constraints.
- **Enrichment only**: expansion involving three or more polynomial factors. Do not use an extra factor merely to make a core item “hard”.
- Plausible errors include omitting `2ab` in a perfect-square expansion, mishandling the signs in `(a-b)(c-d)`, or distributing a multiplier to only one term.

### 2.2 Factorisation
- **Easy**: a single common factor, such as `6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)`; a monic trinomial generated from small positive factors.
- **Medium**: mixed-sign trinomials such as `x^2 - x - 12`; differences of two squares such as `a^2 - 9` and `4x^2 - 25`; quadratic trinomials with leading coefficient 2 or 3.
- **Hard (within core scope)**: two-stage factorisation such as `3x^2 - 12 = 3(x+2)(x-2)`; trinomials generated with a leading coefficient up to 6 and controlled integer factors; taking out `-1` first; instructions that explicitly require complete factorisation.
- **Enrichment only**: finding factors and then solving a quadratic equation using the zero-product property. Solving quadratic equations belongs to Form 4 and must not be presented as core Form 2 content.
- Include irreducible or non-applicable expressions **only** when the stated task is to decide whether an expression can be factorised by the methods learned, to classify expressions, or to justify that no factorisation over the specified number system is available. Do not place an irreducible expression in an ordinary “factorise” item that implies a factorisation exists.
- Plausible errors include stopping after a common factor when further factorisation is possible, swapping signs, confusing `(a-b)^2` with `a^2-b^2`, or proposing factors whose expansion does not reproduce the original expression.

### 2.3 Algebraic expressions and algebraic fractions
- **Easy**: collect like terms after one operation; add or subtract fractions with the same monomial denominator; multiply monomial fractions and simplify.
- **Medium**: unlike denominators with a simple common denominator; multiplication requiring one factorisation before cancellation; division by multiplying by the reciprocal; expand a numerator before collecting terms.
- **Hard (within core scope)**: combined operations with two or three operation types; denominators that must first be factorised; subtraction requiring brackets; a final expression that simplifies only after both expansion and factorisation. Keep coefficients and factor structures controlled so the work tests the intended algebra rather than excessive arithmetic.
- Cancellation is permitted only between common **factors**, never between terms joined by `+` or `-`. For example, `(x^2-9)/(x-3)` simplifies to `x+3` for `x != 3`, but `(x+3)/x` cannot be simplified by cancelling `x`.
- Every generated algebraic-fraction item must record all excluded values from the **original expression**. A cancelled factor does not remove its original restriction. For division, also require the divisor to be defined and non-zero; reject instances for which the reciprocal step creates an overlooked restriction.
- If the prompt asks only for a simplified expression, keep excluded values in the answer metadata or state them in the worked solution. If the prompt explicitly asks for restrictions, require them in the student answer.
- Plausible errors include adding denominators, using an incomplete common denominator, failing to invert the divisor, cancelling terms rather than factors, losing a negative sign, or omitting an excluded value.

## Visual aids
- **NONE** for routine symbolic manipulation.

## Generator notes
- For factorable trinomials, generate **backwards from factors**, such as `(ux+p)(vx+q)`, and expand to obtain the displayed expression. Choose signs deliberately, ensure integer coefficients, and independently re-expand the answer factors to verify exact equality.
- To generate a common-factor-first item, choose the complete factorised form first and expand it. Verify that the proposed outside factor is the intended highest common factor and that the remaining expression has the required next factorisation.
- For algebraic fractions, choose a simplified target and non-zero restrictions first, introduce cancellable factors or compatible denominators, then construct the unsimplified question. Simplify independently and compare symbolically or by substitution at several admissible values.
- Validate denominator restrictions against the original question before cancellation. Sample checks must never use excluded values and do not replace symbolic equivalence checking.
- For an addition or subtraction item, compute the least common denominator from fully factorised denominators. Reject an instance if accidental cancellation makes it substantially easier than its assigned difficulty or if the final form is ambiguous under the answer policy.
- Irreducible cases must declare the coefficient domain being considered (normally integer or rational factors) and appear only in an explicit recognition/classification task. The generator must test irreducibility in that domain rather than infer it from a failed heuristic.
- An instruction to “factorise fully” requires every available core method to be applied. An instruction to “simplify” an algebraic fraction must preserve the original domain restrictions even when a factor disappears.
- Cross-links: Form 2 Ch3 uses these manipulation skills when changing the subject of a formula; Form 4 Ch1 uses factorisation to solve quadratic equations. Do not relocate algebraic fractions to Chapter 3.
