# Form 2, Chapter 11: Isometric Transformations

## Overview
Transformations that preserve size and shape: translation, reflection, and rotation. Students identify objects and images, construct images and recover objects, describe transformations fully, investigate rotational symmetry, and distinguish congruency from similarity. Combined transformations are not Form 2 core content; they are introduced in Form 5 Chapter 5.

## Prerequisites
Form 2 Chapter 7 coordinates; basic geometry and line symmetry (Form 1 Chapters 8–9).

## Topic & subtopic breakdown

| # | Subtopic | Content coverage | Key skills / facts | Typical question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11.1 | Transformations and isometries | Transformation, object, image, correspondence of points; isometric and non-isometric transformations; translation, reflection, and rotation as isometries. | An isometry preserves shape, size, lengths, and angles; its image is congruent to its object. | Identify the object and image; classify a transformation; explain whether a displayed transformation is an isometry. |
| 11.2 | Translation | Translation by a given vector; image of a point or shape; description of the translation between an object and its image; inverse task of recovering an object from its image. | Every point moves the same distance in the same direction; use the column vector notation specified in the question. | Draw or find an image; state a translation vector; work backwards from image coordinates to object coordinates. |
| 11.3 | Reflection | Reflection in a stated line; image and object coordinates; axis of reflection between corresponding points; invariant points on the mirror line. Include horizontal, vertical, and suitable diagonal mirror lines on a grid. | The mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining each point and its image; reflection is its own inverse. | Draw or find an image; determine the mirror line; reconstruct the object from its reflected image. |
| 11.4 | Rotation | Rotation about a stated centre through a stated angle and direction; locating the centre of rotation; invariant centre; inverse task of recovering an object from its image. | A rotation is described in words by its centre, angle, and direction. Corresponding points are equally distant from the centre. Do not assess memorised symbolic coordinate rules for rotation. | Rotate a shape on a grid; describe a rotation fully; find its centre; reverse a rotation to recover the object. |
| 11.5 | Rotational symmetry | Rotational symmetry of plane shapes and designs; angle of rotational symmetry; order of rotational symmetry. | Order is the number of times a figure coincides with itself in one complete turn. The smallest positive angle is `360° ÷ order`. | Determine or justify the order; find the smallest angle; complete or design a figure with a stated order. |
| 11.6 | Congruency and similarity | Identify congruent figures; compare congruency and similarity in relation to transformations. | Congruent figures have the same shape and size. Similar figures have the same shape with corresponding lengths in a common ratio; congruent figures are similar with scale factor 1, but similar figures need not be congruent. Translation, reflection, and rotation preserve congruency. | Match congruent figures; decide whether pairs are congruent, similar only, or neither; explain why an isometric image is congruent to its object. |

## Difficulty strategies

### 11.1 Transformations and isometries
- **Easy**: identify the object, image, or transformation type from one clear diagram.
- **Medium**: identify corresponding vertices and state which properties are preserved.
- **Hard**: justify whether a less familiar mapping shown on a grid is an isometry, using distances and angles rather than appearance alone.

### 11.2 Translation
- **Easy**: translate a point by a given vector on a grid.
- **Medium**: translate a triangle or determine the vector from a labelled object-image pair.
- **Hard**: recover all object vertices from the image and translation vector, using coordinates in more than one quadrant.

### 11.3 Reflection
- **Easy**: reflect a point or simple shape in the x-axis or y-axis.
- **Medium**: reflect in a line such as `x = k` or `y = k`, or find the mirror line from an object-image pair.
- **Hard**: reconstruct an object from its image across a suitable diagonal line, or determine and justify the mirror line using corresponding point pairs.

### 11.4 Rotation
- **Easy**: perform a quarter-turn or half-turn about a marked centre on a grid.
- **Medium**: rotate about a vertex of the shape, or describe a displayed rotation fully in words.
- **Hard**: locate the centre using perpendicular-bisector reasoning, or reverse a rotation about an arbitrary integer grid point to recover the object.
- Suitable distractors include reversing clockwise and anticlockwise, using the wrong centre, confusing object with image, or swapping the components of a translation vector.

### 11.5 Rotational symmetry
- **Easy**: state the order of a familiar regular shape.
- **Medium**: determine the smallest angle from the order, or the order from the smallest angle.
- **Hard**: complete a partially drawn design so that it has exactly a stated order, then justify that no smaller positive turn maps it onto itself.

### 11.6 Congruency and similarity
- **Easy**: identify a congruent pair among clearly different figures.
- **Medium**: classify pairs as congruent, similar only, or neither by comparing corresponding sides and angles.
- **Hard**: use incomplete side or coordinate information to justify the classification, including the fact that scale factor 1 is the congruent case of similarity.

## Visual aids
- **Usually required** for construction, identification, and object-image questions: use a Cartesian grid or accurately constrained geometric diagram with the object solid and the image visually distinct. Label corresponding vertices consistently, for example `P, Q, R` and `P′, Q′, R′`.
- Show or state mirror lines and centres of rotation unambiguously. When students must determine one of them, do not accidentally reveal it through styling or auxiliary marks.
- Rotational-symmetry figures must be generated from a repeated-sector model so the intended order is exact. Do not rely on a roughly drawn decorative image.
- Text or tables may be used for property, angle/order, and congruency-versus-similarity questions when a diagram supplies no necessary information.

## Generator notes
- Generate one transformation at a time for Form 2 core items. Do not use successive or combined transformations, transformation composition notation, or an undefined convention such as "`AB` means apply `B` then `A`". Reserve these for Form 5 Chapter 5.
- Maintain explicit roles for `object` and `image` in the internal model. For inverse questions, generate the object first, transform it to validate the image, and then hide the object from the student prompt.
- Keep grid vertices within the declared bounds before and after transformation. Integer coordinates are preferred, but do not restrict every valid construction to memorised coordinate rules.
- For a translation, state the direction of the column-vector components and check the order of the horizontal and vertical movements.
- For a reflection, verify that the mirror line perpendicularly bisects every object-image segment.
- For a rotation, validate equal distances from the centre and the common directed angle for every corresponding pair. Require a verbal description containing centre, angle, and direction; symbolic coordinate representations of rotation are excluded as an assessment target.
- For rotational symmetry, compare the figure after each divisor-angle turn and ensure the reported order is the smallest repeated sector count that reproduces the whole figure. Treat a full `360°` turn as order 1 for a figure with no non-trivial rotational symmetry.
- Congruency and similarity items must compare corresponding vertices in a declared order or with unambiguous labels. Do not infer correspondence solely from visual orientation.
- Cross-link to Form 2 Chapter 7 for coordinates and Form 1 Chapter 9 for symmetry. Form 5 Chapter 5 extends this chapter to enlargement and combined transformations.
